**Three things the numbers say:**

1. **Winners rank for dozens of phrasings, not one.** McDonald's ranks for 120 keywords, 7UP for 33. The page answers "who owns", "who owned", "owner of", "is X owned by Y" and "does Y own X" all at once.
2. **"Is X owned by Y?" queries are the hidden prize.** Chipotle's best keyword is "is chipotle owned by mcdonald's" (1,900/mo), yet the page sits at #14. A dedicated answer to the parent-company myth would move it.
3. **The AI pages are a different fight.** Higher difficulty (KD 20-47 vs single digits for many brand terms), fast-moving facts, and competitors that update weekly. They need fresher data and stronger primary sources than the brand pages, not the same template.

## Why the winners win

I read the top two pages (McDonald's, 7UP) and our Cursor page, which is newer and ranks #43, and checked the live Google result for "who owns cursor".

**What McDonald's and 7UP do that we should copy everywhere:**

- **The answer is the first sentence.** "McDonald's is owned by its public shareholders" and "KDP in the US, PepsiCo everywhere else". Then names and percentages.
- **They split "ownership" into the questions people actually mean.** McDonald's separates who owns the stock, who owns the restaurants, and who controls the economics (land, leases, royalties). 7UP separates US vs international rights. That split is why one page ranks for 120 phrasings.
- **They are table-heavy.** McDonald's has 4 tables (holders above 5%, restaurant footprint, revenue mix, timeline). 7UP has 5 (snapshot, sister brands, timeline, leadership, ownership layers).
- **They kill a myth by name.** "Is McDonald's owned by Coca-Cola?" and 7UP's "Debunking the common misconception". "Is X owned by Y" searches are often bigger than the main keyword.
- **They have a dated ownership timeline:** 13 rows for McDonald's from the 1940s to Sep 2026; 7UP from 1929 to a planned 2027 split.
- **They show freshness:** "What changed in 2026" near the top, and "What I am watching next" at the end.
- **They cite filings by name:** proxy statements, 13G/A filings, 10-K, 8-K.

**What's holding the section back, including on the winners:**

- **Sources are named but not linked.** McDonald's cites the proxy and 13Gs without a single link. Linking the actual filing is free trust.
- **Almost no contextual internal links.** The McDonald's body has zero; 7UP has two. All linking happens in sidebars and "related posts", which carry little weight.
- **Author is "Max Talks".** It reads like a placeholder and undercuts trust on money topics. Leaders pages use Daniel Mercer; ownership pages should too.
- **Category is "Money", not Ownership.** These should sit in the Ownership hub that is coming with the new site.
- **AI pages lag on freshness.** The Google result for "who owns cursor" shows Cursor's own blog, OpenAI's Aug 28 notice winding down its model contract with Cursor, Forbes, Reddit and Revenue Memo. Fast-moving AI ownership rewards pages updated within days of each event, and primary sources outrank summaries.
- **Timelines are tables only.** Fine for brands with a slow history; AI companies with six rounds in two years need a chart.

## The ideal page

One template for every company, built from what McDonald's and 7UP already prove, plus what they miss. Subtitles carry the company name and a question or number people search. Sections marked "if relevant" are dropped, not padded.

| # | Section | Subtitle pattern | What goes in it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | H1 + hook | Who owns \[Company\]? \[The surprising part\] | e.g. "Who owns 7UP? The confusing split between US and international owners" |
| 2 | The short answer | (boxed, no H2) | First sentence: "\[Company\] is owned by \[owner\]." Then the legal entity, public or private, the as-of date, and the top 2-3 holders with stakes. 40-60 words. |
| 3 | Key takeaways | Key takeaways | 4-5 bullets, each a fact with a number |
| 4 | What changed | What changed in \[year\] | The latest deal, round, IPO filing, CEO change, lawsuit. Dated |
| 5 | Ownership at a glance | Who owns \[Company\] today | Holder table: holder · type (founder, fund, parent, public) · stake or band · voting rights · source · date |
| 6 | Control | Who controls \[Company\]? | Share classes, voting %, board seats, golden shares, trusts. Only if control differs from economics (CoreWeave, Thinking Machines, Anthropic) |
| 7 | The "ownership" split | Who owns the \[restaurants / brand rights / product\]? | The second meaning of ownership people search: franchisees, regional rights, licensed IP (if relevant) |
| 8 | Parent and siblings | What else \[Parent\] owns | Sister brands or portfolio companies, as a table (if there is a parent) |
| 9 | Founders | \[Company\] founders: who started it | Names, roles, published stakes with source; each name links to their Leaders profile |
| 10 | Funding or deal history | \[Company\] funding history / How \[Parent\] bought \[Company\] | Rounds or acquisition terms table: date · amount · valuation · disclosed or reported · lead · source |
| 11 | Ownership timeline | \[Company\] ownership timeline: \[founding year\] to \[current year\] | See the timelines section |
| 12 | The myth | Is \[Company\] owned by \[Y\]? | The most-searched wrong owner, answered directly. One H2 per myth with real volume |
| 13 | The finding | \[What the filing shows\], e.g. "What the 8-K says about the $60B" | The brief's must-do information gain, in first person |
| 14 | Leadership | Who runs \[Company\] | CEO, chair, since when (short table) |
| 15 | FAQ | \[Company\] ownership FAQ | 4-6 People Also Ask questions, 40-60 words each |
| 16 | Watching | What I'm watching next | 2-3 dated upcoming events (IPO, lock-up expiry, pending round) |
| 17 | Sources | Sources | Every link opened, with access date |

Length: 1,500-2,500 words. A simple subsidiary (Snapple) can be 1,200; a contested AI cap table (OpenAI) can run 3,000.

## Ownership timelines

Every page gets a timeline, because "who owned X before" and "when did Y buy X" are some of the most common follow-up searches. The format depends on the company.

**What goes on it (every page):**

- Founding, with founders
- Every change of owner: acquisition, merger, spin-off, IPO, take-private, rights split
- Every funding round or stake sale that changed who holds a meaningful share
- Control events: new share classes, board changes, a founder leaving
- Pending events, clearly marked "announced" or "reported", never as done
- The current state as the last row, with today's date

**One row per event:** date (as exact as the source allows) · event · who owned it after · amount and valuation (disclosed or reported) · source link.

**Pick the format by company type:**

| Company type | Format | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Old consumer brand, few owner changes | Timeline table, oldest first, 8-15 rows | 7UP: 1929 founding to the planned 2027 KDP split |
| Public company with diffuse ownership | Timeline table + a holders table showing today's top holders | McDonald's |
| Venture-backed startup | Valuation chart (one point per priced round, dated, log scale if it spans 10x+) plus the round table below it | Cognition: $350M to $48B in about 30 months |
| Acquired startup | Round table + an "acquisition" row that shows what shareholders received (cash, stock, exchange ratio) | Cursor: rounds, then the SpaceX 8-K |
| Contested or layered (nonprofit, PBC, trusts) | Timeline table + a structure diagram of who sits above whom | OpenAI: nonprofit, PBC, Microsoft, Foundation warrant |

**Rules:**

- Valuations on a chart only when a round was priced. Talks and estimates go in the table, labeled, never on the line.
- Never interpolate between rounds or extend the line into the future.
- Write the reading in first person under the chart: "I count six priced rounds in 30 months. The jump came after..."
- Real HTML tables, never images of tables.
- Find old ownership in: SEC filings (S-1 history section, 10-K "Item 1"), Companies House filing history, the company's own "about" or history page, Wayback Machine captures, and contemporaneous news for the deal itself.

### Winning AI answers and snippets

- The first sentence must stand alone as a complete answer. AI Overviews lift it word for word.
- Holder and timeline data in real tables, so Google can read rows.
- Put a dated number next to every claim. AI answers favor sources that state dates.
- Link primary filings. The Cursor AI Overview cites Forbes, CNBC and Wikipedia, all of which cite filings; citing the filing directly makes us the better source.

## Research, new information and voice

**Where ownership lives, by company type:**

| Company type | Records to open first |
| --- | --- |
| US public company | Latest DEF 14A proxy (beneficial ownership table), 13G/13D filings, 10-K Item 1 and exhibits for share classes, Form 4s for insider sales |
| Subsidiary of a public company | Parent's 10-K (segment and subsidiary list, Exhibit 21), acquisition 8-K, parent's investor relations |
| US private startup | SEC Form D for the company and for SPVs named after it, company funding announcements, Forbes/Bloomberg founder stake estimates, court records |
| UK company | Companies House: persons with significant control, share allotments (SH01), confirmation statements, annual accounts |
| Acquired startup | Buyer's 8-K or merger agreement, buyer's 10-Q note on the acquisition, the company's own announcement |
| Nonprofit or PBC structures | Form 990s, the company's structure page, state filings, court records |

**Every page needs at least one finding nobody else has.** The reliable ones:

1. **The exact number from a filing** that summaries round off (Databricks' $4,999,997,255 from 136 investors).
2. **Votes vs economics** when they differ (CoreWeave founders hold \~72% of votes on a small share of stock).
3. **The legal entity name** nobody uses (Sierra Technologies, Inc.; Windsurf's Exafunction, Inc.).
4. **The myth, checked against the record** (Meta's 10-Q says "non-voting minority" and never says 49%).
5. **SPVs and retail owners** visible in Form D and Form C filings.
6. **What changed since the ranking pages were written:** a new filing, a closed deal, an exit.

**Voice:** first-person investigator, as Daniel Mercer, the same as the Leaders pages. "I opened the proxy." "I couldn't find a Form D under that name." "My read:" before analysis. "I" only for research steps actually done; never claim interviews or contacts. Full voice rules and examples are in the Leaders Content Playbook.

**Accuracy rules:**

- Separate who holds the shares, who holds the votes, and what the company is worth.
- "Reported" vs "disclosed" on every valuation and stake. A Companies House band (75%+) is a band, not a stake.
- Talks are talks. A pending deal is never written as done.
- Link the record itself, not a summary of it.

## Interlinking, refresh and checklist

### Interlinking

The winners link almost nothing from their body text. That is the cheapest gain available across the whole section.

1. **Founders and executives** link to their Leaders profile on first mention.
2. **Parent and sibling companies** link to their own "Who owns" page (7UP to Dr Pepper and Keurig; Cognition to Cursor and Replit; DeepMind to Isomorphic).
3. **Myth targets** link out: "Is Chipotle owned by McDonald's?" links to Who owns McDonald's.
4. **Salary pages** where they exist ("what OpenAI pays").
5. **Comparison pages** where they exist (Coke vs Pepsi vs Dr Pepper brand map).
6. **Every new page gets at least three links in from existing pages** on publish day. Update the older pages, not just the new one.
7. Anchor text = the target's question or name ("who owns Dr Pepper", "Brett Adcock's net worth"). Never "here". One link per target per page; 5-12 in-body links.

### Refresh rules

| Trigger | Action | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acquisition, IPO, funding round, CEO change, ruling | Update short answer, holders, timeline, "What changed" | Within 7 days |
| New proxy or 13G (public companies) | Refresh the holder table | Within 30 days of filing |
| AI and other fast-moving private companies | Full fact check | Monthly |
| Stable consumer brands | Full fact check | Quarterly |
| Page drops 5+ positions | Compare against the pages that passed it, fill the gap | Within 2 weeks |

Every refresh updates the visible date and dateModified. Never change the URL.
