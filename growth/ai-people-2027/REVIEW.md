Reviewed 13 October 2026. Status: development proposal, not publication approval.

## Recommendation

Build a flagship watchlist about the people behind consequential AI businesses, research and infrastructure. Its distinctive value should be a documented contribution, an economic consequence and an observable 2027 test for every person. The supplied draft is a useful research intake, but it needs a substantial edit and a complete claim review before release.

Suggested H1: **100 AI People to Watch in 2027—and the Bets Behind Them**.

Suggested title tag: **100 AI People to Watch in 2027 | Visionary Talks**.

Suggested meta: **Meet 100 AI researchers, engineers and founders through their documented work, the businesses behind it and the milestones to watch in 2027.**

Keep the proposed `/ai-people-to-watch/` canonical. Show “2027 edition · Evidence checked through [actual completion date]” and explain that this is a prospective watchlist, not a ranking. Do not date it 2027 before publication or claim that the entire roster was verified on this review date.

## Changes needed before writing the final article

1. Replace the opening claims that these are people readers do not know, that most have no Wikipedia page, or that half the industry runs their projects. The roster contains recognizable founders and media personalities; the draft does not establish those broad statements.
2. Remove the claim that every paper trail has already been followed. First-person research claims must match the work completed. After the remaining checks, describe precisely which records were examined and the limitations.
3. Cut repetitive degree lists, career chronology and “my read” restatements. Education is optional context, not an obligatory pedigree contest. Never infer a degree from an athletics roster or authorship from a project affiliation.
4. Remove causal bridges that the evidence does not establish. A person contributing to one reasoning project does not prove involvement in a later mathematics result. Team accomplishments require individual attribution.
5. Separate current employment from historical employment. Record both the effective date of a role and the date its source was accessed. An undated current staff page does not establish the promotion date.
6. Keep company financing, valuation, acquisition consideration, revenue, ARR and personal wealth in different fields. A startup's funding is not a founder's wealth or compensation.
7. Deduplicate company events. Liam Fedus and Ekin Cubuk share Periodic Labs; Chelsea Finn and Karol Hausman share Physical Intelligence. Do not sum a company's round once per person.
8. Remove the demographic conclusion inferred from this editorial selection. Do not infer gender from names or portraits; a nonrepresentative roster cannot establish who receives funding across the industry.
9. Keep watch tests explicitly editorial. Company promises, product announcements and self-reported benchmarks are evidence of statements, not independent confirmation of delivery or commercial success.
10. Keep safety, policy, investing and media, but explain their different mechanisms of influence. They should not all be described as engineers building models.

## The article's editorial argument

The strongest thesis is: **the next AI businesses depend on people solving specific bottlenecks, and public records let us distinguish those bets from their publicity.**

Organize the introduction around four questions, with sourced examples rather than sweeping industry conclusions:

- Who is reducing the cost of serving models, and what survives as open infrastructure?
- Who is converting research into physical experiments, usable software or repeat customer revenue?
- Which announcements have shipped, and which are still promises?
- Who establishes the tests, governance and distribution on which these businesses depend?

The opened seed announcements illustrate commercialization, but three selected firms do not establish an industry-wide funding trend. The ggml statement supplies a counterexample to a simplistic “open source becomes private” story. The Base44 deal terms show why transaction headlines are poor proxies for founder wealth.

## Recommended reading flow

| Order | Module | Reader value |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title, edition date, Daniel byline, 100–150-word introduction | Scope and reason to read; no unsupported superiority claim. |
| 2 | Four or five documentary findings | Original reporting value before the long roster. |
| 3 | Interactive “Where the people fit” map | Explore capabilities and business bottlenecks, with a table alternative. |
| 4 | Searchable 100-person index | Find a name, employer, project or field and jump to a stable anchor. |
| 5 | Five chapter sections, 12 featured entries and 88 short entries | Read selectively without losing the complete list. |
| 6 | Capital and operating-evidence tables | Inspect definitions, dates, sources and company-level deduplication. |
| 7 | 2027 watch tests | Understand what would strengthen or weaken each thesis. |
| 8 | Selection method, limitations, corrections and update log | Make the work accountable and refreshable. |

Retain the draft's five chapters for continuity. Add cross-cutting tags for inference, agents, physical AI, science, enterprise adoption and assurance. A person has one chapter but can have multiple tags. Avoid category churn just to make every section the same size.

Feature about 12 people at 180–250 words each and use 70–110 words for the remaining entries. This is a working editing budget, not a word-count publication requirement; approximately 10,000–14,000 words should be achievable including the analysis and tables. If an entry cannot support a distinct contribution and a credible watch thesis, replace the candidate or publish a smaller, honestly titled list. Do not pad to reach 100.

## A repeatable entry flow

**[Name]: [specific bottleneck or documented contribution]**

1. **Role and contribution:** dated current role with a primary source, then the specific artifact or outcome and the person's attributable contribution.
2. **Financial consequence:** my short analysis of cost, distribution, ownership, reliability, adoption or capital requirements. Analysis must follow the evidence.
3. **2027 test:** one observable milestone and one risk or disconfirming result. Identify these as my watch criteria, not a company forecast.
4. Optional concise context: education, previous work or transaction detail only when it explains the current bet.

Link evidence naturally inside these sentences. Keep granular citations in the research ledger even when the public entry has only two or three useful links. Do not repeat “not verified” several times within a short entry; remove unnecessary claims and record unresolved work in the audit.

### Sample voice, ready for editorial review

**Georgi Gerganov: can a commercial sponsor preserve open inference?**

I am watching the relationship between open infrastructure and the companies funding it. In the [ggml team's February announcement](https://huggingface.co/blog/ggml-joins-hf), joining Hugging Face came with a stated commitment to retain technical direction and community autonomy. That distinction matters: institutional backing does not, by itself, turn a community project into a proprietary product. My 2027 test is whether public releases, contribution rules and project stewardship continue to support that commitment. An acquisition price or Gerganov's personal proceeds is not disclosed in the record I examined.

**Liam Fedus and Ekin Cubuk: what $300M needs to produce**

The evidence here starts with [a16z's $300M founding-round announcement](https://a16z.com/announcement/investing-in-periodic-labs/), not a personal-wealth estimate. Periodic's [launch statement](https://periodic.com/news/introducing-periodic-labs) proposes a loop between AI and physical laboratory experiments. I would judge that bet by reproducible experimental results and evidence that the loop improves the cost or speed of useful research. Funding buys the opportunity to attempt that work; it does not prove the scientific or commercial outcome. The company event belongs once in the financial ledger even though both founders are on the list.

**Misha Laskin: the difference between announcing and delivering**

Reflection's [5 October Beam post](https://reflection.ai/blog/introducing-beam) announced the model while promising its weights and supporting artifacts later that month. I would keep those stages separate. My 2027 test begins with accessible artifacts, clear license terms and independent reproduction of important results, then asks whether the model offers useful serving economics. Reflection's own performance tables provide claims to investigate; they are not an independent benchmark.

### 3. A documentary timeline

For selected projects, show source-linked paper publication → public code/model release → company launch → financing or documented deployment. Use discrete dated events. ### 4. A portrait treatment with an explicit editorial purpose

Use a contact sheet of 8–12 featured people for the hero and compact portraits for those featured entries. Match the existing Leaders illustration system; warm paper, black ink and muted sage/blue/ochre chapter accents. Label each generated likeness **AI illustration**, with descriptive alt text; do not present it as a photograph or evidence. Use verified reference identities and suitable cleared assets. Defer image generation until the featured roster is verified, so an image does not lock in a weak candidate.

Use five simple code-native chapter illustrations for the rest rather than generating 100 portraits. Reserve dimensions, provide responsive compressed assets, load below-fold imagery lazily and keep the hero visually legible as a social image. No generated numbers, document facsimiles or graphs embedded in illustrations.

An accurately re-plotted METR chart could explain why evaluators matter, but the opened page is a historical version and explicitly no longer updated. ## Tables: the actual information architecture

| Table | Columns | Editorial rule |
|---|---|---|
| People index | Name; dated role/organization; chapter; documented contribution; 2027 test | Real HTML, stable anchors, searchable; roster order is not rank. |
| Company capital ledger | Company ID; event; amount; currency; date; valuation/basis; source; status | One row per company event. Separate priced financing, tender, debt and acquisition. |
| Operating evidence | Company; metric; value; period; definition; reported/estimated; source | Group ARR, annualized revenue, recognized revenue and users separately. No mixed-metric leaderboard. |
| Watch tests | Person/project; current evidence; observable milestone; countercase; review trigger | Editorial criteria, not probabilities or guaranteed forecasts. |
| Update log | Date; changed claim; evidence; editor | Only substantive reviewed changes update dateModified. |

Do not add a 100-person net-worth table. Most researchers do not have a credible public estimate. Where wealth matters, link to an existing reviewed Leaders profile and preserve the independent estimator/date/methodology there.

## Implementation fit for this repository

Create one watchlist feature contract and renderer, not 100 independent page layouts. Suggested contract identifier: `ai-watchlist-v1`. Reuse the article shell, Daniel's byline, existing source/citation helpers, shared EditorialChart and real table styles. The conceptual preview in this folder is not an alternate production template.

Person fields: stable ID; display name and aliases; chapter; discovery tags; role and effective date; role source; contribution and artifact sources; narrative; editorial watch test and countercase; company IDs; existing profile URL when verified; optional portrait asset; claim/source IDs; last checked date; review state.

Event fields: stable company/event IDs; date; event type; currency; amount; valuation with pre/post/unknown basis; completion state; source IDs and evidence scope. Unknowns are null. A single company event can relate to multiple people without duplication.

Source fields: URL; publisher; title; publication/effective date; accessed date; locator; supported claims; issuer/independent classification; qualifications. A record can verify funding while leaving the founder's title pending. Do not collapse those into a single “verified person” badge.

Use `/ai-people-to-watch/#georgi-gerganov` style anchors. Filters should be keyboard-operable and announce result counts; no-JavaScript readers retain the full roster. Table headers and captions remain visible. Render every entry on the server, even if details are initially collapsed. Avoid infinite-scroll-only discovery or charts that hide their data from assistive technology.

Use Article and Breadcrumb metadata matching the visible page. The feature itself is not a single-person ProfilePage. Verify all related URLs before adding links; find published profiles, ownership stories and salary pages in the current index rather than constructing presumed slugs. Do not imply FAQ rich results or guaranteed ranking. Add contextual links from the Leaders/AI tracker hubs only after publication approval.
