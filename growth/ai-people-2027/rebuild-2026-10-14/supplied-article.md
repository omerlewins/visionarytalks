# 100 AI People to Watch in 2027: The Engineers, Researchers and Founders Building What Comes Next

*Suggested URL: /ai-people-to-watch/ · SEO title: 100 AI People to Watch in 2027: Engineers, Researchers and Founders · Meta description: 100 AI researchers, engineers, founders and innovators to watch in 2027, with their education, career history, dated funding figures and sources.*

The 100 people below aren't the ones you already know. They wrote the paper the famous model was built on, maintain the open-source project that half the industry runs on, or grew a startup from zero to nine figures of revenue while the press was looking elsewhere. Most have no Wikipedia page. A few have a Forbes estimate. All of them have a paper trail, and I followed it.

I put this list together the way I build every Leaders profile on this site. I start with records: university pages, papers, company filings, funding announcements. Every figure carries its date. A company's valuation is never treated as a person's wealth. Where I couldn't verify something, the entry says so. That happens more often than a typical "AI influencers" list would admit, and I think that's the point.

It's organized into five sections: frontier researchers, infrastructure engineers, founders, applied AI by industry, and the people in safety, policy, investing and media who shape how everyone else understands the field. Numbering follows section order. It is not a ranking.

## What the 100 tell me

Read all 100 entries together and five patterns stand out. Each one is a guess about where the money and the talent go next.

**1. Leaving a lab now means leaving with capital.** Jerry Tworek left OpenAI in January 2026 and had founded Core Automation by March. Liam Fedus co-founded Periodic Labs and raised a $300M seed. Misha Laskin has said on CNBC that Reflection AI raised at a $25B pre-money valuation. Sara Hooker left Cohere and raised a reported $50M seed for Adaption. Lin Junyang left Qwen and launched Pragmatik Labs with several hundred million dollars behind it. A few years ago, a senior researcher's next step was another lab. Now it's often a cap table.

**2. Open-source projects are becoming companies.** vLLM turned into Inferact, which raised a $150M seed at an $800M valuation (Jan 2026). SGLang spun out as RadixArk with a $100M seed at $400M. The team behind llama.cpp joined Hugging Face in February 2026. A popular GitHub repository has turned out to be a strong signal of an investable company.

**3. Revenue shows up faster, and a lot of it is never disclosed.** Lovable reported $4M ARR four weeks after launch and says it was tracking toward a $600M run rate by August 2026. ElevenLabs passed $500M ARR in May 2026. Meanwhile, ten of the 20 founders' companies don't disclose revenue or ARR at all. Where a number exists, I give it with its date. Where one doesn't, I say so rather than repeat an estimate as fact.

**4. China's top researchers are building inside China.** Luo Fuli went from DeepSeek to running Xiaomi's MiMo team. Yao Shunyu, the researcher behind ReAct, became Tencent's Chief AI Scientist in December 2025. Moonshot AI, founded by Yang Zhilin, was valued at $50B in October 2026 and has filed confidentially for a Hong Kong IPO targeting early 2027.

**5. Robots, molecules and materials are the next magnet.** Physical Intelligence was reported in talks at roughly $11B. Periodic Labs was reported in talks at about $7.5B. Isomorphic Labs' new president took over on January 1, 2026. None of those reported rounds was confirmed closed when I checked, and the entries label them that way.

One gap I won't paper over: 20 of the 100 are women. I chose people for what they've built and what the record shows, and I didn't pad the list to change the ratio. That number says something about who gets funded and credited in AI right now. I'll keep watching it in next year's edition.

## Frontier AI researchers

### 1. Alexander Wei
**Research Scientist, OpenAI** · Frontier research

*Illustration: [AI EDITORIAL ILLUSTRATION: Alexander Wei]*

In July 2025, a small OpenAI team led by Alexander Wei produced an experimental model that scored at gold-medal level on the International Mathematical Olympiad: 35 of 42 points, five of six problems solved. It now anchors OpenAI's math-reasoning push.

**Education:** A.B. and S.M., Harvard (2020), advised by Jelani Nelson and Scott Kominers. Ph.D. in Computer Science, UC Berkeley (2023), advised by Nika Haghtalab, Michael I. Jordan and Jacob Steinhardt. Meta Research PhD Fellow (2022 to 2023) and NSF Graduate Research Fellow (2020 to 2023).

**The path:** Wei won IOI gold in 2015 and a SODA 2019 Best Student Paper award. At Meta FAIR he co-authored CICERO, the Diplomacy-playing AI published in Science in 2022. He was at OpenAI by 2025 (start date not verified). On July 19, 2025, he and Noam Brown announced the IMO result, crediting general-purpose reinforcement learning and test-time compute scaling rather than task-specific methods.

**Why I'm watching in 2027:** OpenAI's stated internal goal is a fully autonomous AI researcher by March 2028, and its agent systems claimed a Navier-Stokes result in September 2026 that is still under review. No source names Wei on that work; his IMO-gold methods sit at the root of OpenAI's broader math-reasoning push.

**My read:** Wei described a model that can "craft intricate, watertight arguments at the level of human mathematicians." His role is confirmed only by his personal site (Oct 2026), so recheck it before print.

**Sources:** [alexwei.org](https://www.alexwei.org/) · [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-claims-a-breakthrough-in-llm-reasoning-on-complex-math-problems/) · [Meta Research Fellows](https://research.facebook.com/fellows/wei-alexander/) · [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/openai-noam-brown-ai-agents-agi/)

### 2. Jerry Tworek
**Founder, Core Automation** · Frontier research

*Illustration: [AI EDITORIAL ILLUSTRATION: Jerry Tworek]*

The press calls him the "father of reasoning models." In January 2026 Jerry Tworek walked away from OpenAI, where he led the o1 effort, and has said deep learning research as currently practiced "is done."

**Education:** Master's in mathematics, University of Warsaw, on an individual curriculum focused on financial mathematics. Year not verified; secondary source (aiwiki).

**The path:** Per aiwiki, he spent about five years building futures strategies, rising to head of R&D at a hedge fund. At OpenAI from 2019 he worked on the Rubik's Cube robot hand, then Codex (the basis of GitHub Copilot) and GSM8K verifiers in 2021 and 2022. Promoted to VP of Research in 2022, he did GPT-4 post-training (2023) and led o1 (Sept 2024) and o3. He left in early January 2026 "to try and explore types of research that are hard to do at OpenAI," and founded Core Automation in March 2026.

**Why I'm watching in 2027:** Core Automation wants to be "the most automated AI lab in the world," betting on learning beyond pretraining and RL and on architectures that outscale transformers. It raised $100M at a $1B valuation, then reportedly sought $300M to $500M at about $4B within weeks (The Information, spring 2026; reported, not confirmed closed). Nvidia is named as an investor.

**My read:** A flagship continual-learning project, reportedly codenamed "Ceres," aims for a roughly 100x cut in training data (secondary source). That number is the whole thesis.

**Sources:** [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/news/story/openai-reasoning-chief-jerry-tworek-quits-after-7-years-heres-why-509827-2026-01-07) · [The Decoder: launch](https://the-decoder.com/ex-openai-researcher-jerry-tworek-launches-core-automation-to-build-the-most-automated-ai-lab-in-the-world/) · [The Decoder: valuation](https://the-decoder.com/ai-money-keeps-flowing-as-deepseek-plans-record-raise-and-core-automation-quadruples-valuation-in-weeks/) · [aiwiki](https://aiwiki.ai/wiki/jerry_tworek)

### 3. Jason Wei
**Researcher, Meta Superintelligence Labs** · Frontier research

*Illustration: [AI EDITORIAL ILLUSTRATION: Jason Wei]*

Two years out of undergrad, as a Google Brain resident, Jason Wei was lead author on the chain-of-thought prompting paper, one of the most-cited papers of the LLM era. Meta's challenge now is keeping him.

**Education:** B.A. in computer science, Dartmouth College (2020). Goldwater Scholar (2019). No PhD.

**The path:** Wei joined Google Brain as an AI Resident in October 2020 and stayed as a research engineer and research scientist until February 2023. There he led "Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models" (NeurIPS 2022) and worked on FLAN instruction tuning (ICLR 2022) and "Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models" (2022). At OpenAI, from February 2023 to July 2025, he worked on o-series reasoning and Deep Research and built the SimpleQA (2024) and BrowseComp (2025) benchmarks. He moved to Meta Superintelligence Labs in July 2025.

**Why I'm watching in 2027:** MSL shipped its first foundation model, Muse Spark, in April 2026, then suffered heavy researcher attrition, including multimodal head Jiahui Yu in August 2026. Whether Wei stays and shapes Meta's next reasoning model is a clean test of Meta's talent bet.

**My read:** Meta reportedly offered some top researchers packages of up to $300M over four years; Wei's terms were never disclosed. I found no fresh 2026 confirmation that he is still at Meta, so his title needs a check before print.

### 6. Joshua (Josh) Batson
**Research Scientist, Interpretability, Anthropic** · Frontier research

*Illustration: [AI EDITORIAL ILLUSTRATION: Joshua (Josh) Batson]*

"Inside the model, it's just a bunch of numbers," Josh Batson told VentureBeat in March 2025. His job is reading those numbers, and his team's work showed Claude planning rhymes ahead and sometimes fabricating its reasoning.

**Education:** B.A. in mathematics, Yale (Phi Beta Kappa), where he founded the Yale Undergraduate Math Society and won six prizes, five in mathematics and one in literary criticism. Part III of the Mathematical Tripos, Churchill College, Cambridge, as a Gates Cambridge Scholar (2008). Ph.D. in mathematics, MIT (2014), advised by Peter Ozsváth; thesis "Obstructions to slicing knots and splitting links."

**The path:** Batson started as a knot theorist. He moved into machine learning through self-supervised denoising, "Noise2Self," with Loic Royer (2019); a Chan Zuckerberg Biohub affiliation is reported, but I could not verify it from a primary page, nor his Anthropic start date. In March 2025 he co-authored "On the Biology of a Large Language Model," Anthropic's attribution-graph and circuit-tracing study, and the company open-sourced the tools. He appeared in a 60 Minutes segment in November 2025.

**Why I'm watching in 2027:** Interpretability is moving from research curiosity to audit tool. As regulators start asking how models reason, Batson is one of the clearest explainers of what attribution graphs can and cannot show.

**My read:** The literary-criticism prize is not trivia. Translating model internals for non-specialists is half this job.

**Sources:** [Math Genealogy Project](https://genealogy.math.ndsu.nodak.edu/id.php?id=189873) · [Gates Cambridge](https://gatescambridge.org/biography/6120) · [Transformer Circuits](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/biology.html) · [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/anthropic-scientists-expose-how-ai-actually-thinks-and-discover-it-secretly-plans-ahead-and-sometimes-lies)

## How I built this list

I researched this edition in October 2026 and checked facts as of October 13, 2026.

- **Who qualified:** People who built something you can verify (a paper, a model, a codebase, a company, a law, a fund) and who have a concrete, dated reason to matter in 2027. I chose people with a smaller public profile over famous ones.
- **Who I left out:** Household names, and anyone who already has a profile in the Leaders index.
- **Sources:** I used primary records first: university and lab pages, papers, company announcements and filings, and official bios. Reputable press filled the gaps. Every entry links to its sources.
- **How I handle figures:** Every figure carries a date. Revenue and performance numbers a company published itself are labeled company-reported. Funding rounds reported as talks are labeled "reported, not confirmed closed." I never treat a company's valuation as a person's wealth.
- **What I didn't verify:** Some entries are missing a graduation year, a degree or an exact title. When I couldn't confirm something, I say so in the entry rather than fill the gap.
- **Corrections:** People in AI change jobs fast. If something here is out of date, the [corrections page](https://visionarytalks.com/corrections/) is the quickest way to reach me.
