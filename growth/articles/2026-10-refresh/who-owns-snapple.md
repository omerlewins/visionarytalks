# Who Owns Snapple? Keurig Dr Pepper, and What the Coming Split Changes

**By Daniel Mercer | Checked October 25, 2026**

Snapple is owned by Keurig Dr Pepper (Nasdaq: KDP), which has owned it outright since the 2018 Keurig merger. KDP plans to spin off its coffee business in early 2027. Snapple stays with the beverage company, alongside Dr Pepper, 7UP and Canada Dry.

There is no Snapple stock and no outside investor in the brand. KDP's 10-Q for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 lists Snapple in its North American refreshment beverage portfolio, next to Dr Pepper, Canada Dry, Mott's, A&W, Peñafiel, GHOST, 7UP, Clamato and Core Hydration.

## What the Keurig Dr Pepper split changes

KDP is in the middle of a two-step plan, and Snapple sits on the side that does not move.

| Step | What happened | Date | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plan announced | KDP said it would buy JDE Peet's and then separate its beverage and coffee businesses into two public companies | August 25, 2025 | KDP 10-Q |
| JDE Peet's bought | KDP paid €31.85 a share in cash; 96.22% of shares were tendered by March 27, for about €14.86 billion | Settled April 1, 2026 | KDP 8-K |
| Coffee CEO named | Russ Torres appointed future CEO of "Global Coffee Co.", effective November 3, 2026 | Announced October 1, 2026 | KDP 8-K |
| Separation | Tax-free spin-off of the coffee business, "expected to occur in early 2027, subject to market and other conditions" | Not yet done, as of October 25, 2026 | KDP 10-Q |

The working names are "Global Coffee Co." (Keurig, Jacobs, L'OR, Peet's) and "Beverage Co." KDP's 2026 proxy says those names stand "pending the announcement of official corporate names." Because the plan is a spin-off of coffee, the existing KDP keeps the soda, juice, water and tea brands, Snapple included. KDP's 10-Q also warns that the board could still change or drop the separation, so treat early 2027 as a target, not a fact.

For Snapple, the practical change is focus. Today it shares a parent with K-Cup pods and Peet's coffee. After the split its parent will be a pure drinks company, where Snapple competes for attention with Dr Pepper, Canada Dry and energy brand GHOST instead of with coffee.

## Who owns Keurig Dr Pepper?

KDP is a public company, so Snapple's ultimate owners are KDP's shareholders. KDP's 2026 proxy listed four holders of more than 5% of its common stock as of April 20, 2026:

| Holder | Shares | Stake |
|---|---|---|
| FMR LLC (Fidelity) | 135,874,927 | 10.0% |
| Capital World Investors | 99,479,243 | 7.3% |
| BlackRock | 93,075,153 | 6.8% |
| Harris Associates | 86,269,175 | 6.3% |

JAB, the investment group with stakes in [Krispy Kreme](/who-owns-krispy-kreme/), [Peet's](/who-owns-peets-coffee/) and [Panera](/who-owns-panera-bread/), was a more than 5% holder until May 5, 2025, the proxy says. KKR and Apollo affiliates bought $4.5 billion of KDP convertible preferred stock on March 30, 2026 to help pay for JDE Peet's. That is preferred stock, not common shares, so it does not appear in the table above.

Tim Cofer has been KDP's CEO since April 26, 2024. Pamela Patsley is board chair. Bob Gamgort, who ran KDP after the 2018 merger, left the board on March 31, 2026.

The 2000 price is often quoted as $1.45 billion. That figure includes the cash, the assumed debt and Snapple stock options that Cadbury took over, which Triarc valued at $123.6 million.

## Who bought Snapple and ruined it?

The buyer people mean is Quaker Oats. Quaker bought Snapple Beverage Corp. for a tender-offer price of $1.7 billion on December 6, 1994, and its own filings show how fast it went wrong.

- **First season:** Quaker's 10-K for the year to June 30, 1995 called Snapple "the largest [acquisition] in the Company's history" and said its performance "has fallen short of expectations." Snapple ran an operating loss in its first seven months under Quaker.
- **The distribution problem:** Snapple reached stores through independent beverage distributors, not Quaker's own system. Quaker said its efforts to fix advertising, merchandising "and distributor communications" were "neither sufficient nor quick enough" for the 1995 season.
- **1996:** US and Canadian Snapple sales fell 8% on an 11% volume decline, according to Quaker's 1996 10-K, which said Snapple "never gained necessary volume momentum early in the beverage season."
- **The exit:** Quaker sold all of Snapple to Triarc for $300 million on May 22, 1997, and booked a pretax loss of $1.41 billion on the sale.

That loss is why the Quaker years are remembered as a textbook bad deal. Triarc kept Snapple for three years, then sold it to Cadbury Schweppes in October 2000, together with the Royal Crown soda business, for $896.3 million in cash plus $425.1 million of assumed debt.

## Is Snapple owned by Coca-Cola or PepsiCo?

No. Snapple has never been owned by Coca-Cola or PepsiCo. Its owner, Keurig Dr Pepper, competes with both in soft drinks. For how the three companies split up the soda aisle, see [Coke vs Pepsi vs Dr Pepper](/coke-vs-pepsi-vs-dr-pepper/).
