# Who Owns Krispy Kreme? JAB Still Controls It With 43% (2026)

**By Daniel Mercer | Checked October 25, 2026**

Krispy Kreme, Inc. (Nasdaq: DNUT) is a public company controlled by JAB, which held 43.03% of its shares as of April 30, 2026, per JAB's June 2026 Schedule 13D/A. Josh Charlesworth is CEO.

Krispy Kreme is public, but it is not widely held. JAB owns enough stock to act as the control layer above the company, and Krispy Kreme's own 10-K for fiscal 2025 warns that JAB's roughly 43% stake "could hinder, delay, or prevent a change in control."

## Who owns Krispy Kreme right now?

| Owner | Stake | Date and source |
|---|---|---|
| JAB (through JAB Indulgence B.V.) | 74,190,990 shares, 43.03% | As of April 30, 2026; Schedule 13D/A Amendment No. 15, filed June 16, 2026 |
| Public shareholders, including index funds and other institutions | About 57% | Remainder of about 172.4 million shares outstanding at April 30, 2026 |

The 13D/A names a chain of JAB entities as beneficial owners: JAB Indulgence B.V., JAB Holdings B.V., JAB Investments S.a r.l., JAB Holding Company S.a r.l., Joh. A. Benckiser B.V., Agnaten SE and Lucresca SE. Krispy Kreme's 2026 proxy ties JAB's controlling entities to the descendants of Albert Reimann, the German family behind JAB.

JAB is not selling. In the same June 2026 filing it reported no change in shares, and said that on June 12, 2026 it extended a cash-settled equity swap with HSBC Continental Europe on Krispy Kreme stock, first signed August 11, 2023, to August 10, 2028. The filing says JAB's exposure "remains unchanged."

## Who runs Krispy Kreme?

Josh Charlesworth has been President and CEO, and a board member, since January 2024, per the company's 2026 proxy. He is leading a turnaround plan, announced in August 2025, that hands markets to franchisees and pays down debt.

## What changed in 2026: the shops are moving to franchisees

The company is keeping its shareholders and giving away its stores. The numbers from Krispy Kreme's second-quarter results, released August 6, 2026:

| Measure | Figure | Period |
|---|---|---|
| Systemwide sales from franchised locations | About 42% | Currently, as of August 6, 2026 |
| Same measure a year earlier | About 25% | Fiscal 2025 |
| Company goal | About 50% | Beginning fiscal 2027 |
| Net revenue | $331.0 million, down 12.8% | Quarter ended June 28, 2026 |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $28.8 million, up 43.2% | Same quarter |
| GAAP net loss | $19.8 million | Same quarter |

Two deals drove it. On March 2, 2026 Krispy Kreme sold 100% of Krispy Kreme Japan for $69.3 million in gross cash, according to its 10-Q. In the same quarter it cut its stake in W.K.S. Krispy Kreme, its western US joint venture, to a minority interest. It also still owns 34.7% of Insomnia Cookies, after selling control of the cookie chain in the third quarter of 2024.

## Is Krispy Kreme owned by Shaq?

No. Shaquille O'Neal never owned Krispy Kreme the company. In October 2016 Krispy Kreme said he had become "an owner of one of its downtown Atlanta franchises," the shop on Ponce de Leon Avenue, the Associated Press reported. No price was disclosed.

That is a stake in a restaurant, not in the company. O'Neal does not appear among the holders in Krispy Kreme's SEC filings, and the controlling shareholder in 2016, as now, was JAB.

## Who originally owned Krispy Kreme?

Vernon Rudolph. He made the first Krispy Kreme doughnuts in Winston-Salem, North Carolina on July 13, 1937, selling mostly to grocery stores until customers came to the door for hot doughnuts. The ownership changes since, as recorded in the company's SEC filings:

| Year | Owner | What happened | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1937 | Vernon Rudolph | First Krispy Kreme doughnuts made in Winston-Salem | Krispy Kreme Doughnuts 10-K, 2015 |
| 1976 | Beatrice Foods | After Rudolph's death, the company became a wholly owned Beatrice subsidiary | Same |
| 1982 | A group of franchisees | Bought the company back from Beatrice | Same |
| 2000 | Public shareholders | IPO, trading as KKD | Same |
| 2016 | JAB | Bought every share for $21.00 in cash; the merger closed July 27, 2016 and the stock left the NYSE | Krispy Kreme Doughnuts 8-K |
| 2021 | Public shareholders, with JAB in control | New holding company Krispy Kreme, Inc. went public at $17.00 a share on Nasdaq as DNUT; JAB owned about 76% right after the IPO, before handing about 38% to its minority partners | IPO prospectus |
| 2026 | JAB, 43.03% | Latest 13D/A | JAB Schedule 13D/A |

The franchisee buyout in 1982 is the one that shaped the brand. The new owners made the hot doughnut experience the priority, and that led to the glass-walled "Doughnut Theater" where customers watch the line, the company's history says.

## Krispy Kreme and the rest of JAB

Krispy Kreme is one piece of a JAB food and coffee portfolio. Keurig Dr Pepper's 2026 proxy describes JAB as having held controlling or significant investments in Peet's, Krispy Kreme and Panera Brands, and JAB was a more than 5% holder of Keurig Dr Pepper itself until May 5, 2025.

## Can you buy Krispy Kreme stock?

Yes. Krispy Kreme, Inc. trades on Nasdaq under DNUT. Buying shares makes you a minority holder alongside JAB, which with 43.03% can heavily influence board elections and any sale of the company.
