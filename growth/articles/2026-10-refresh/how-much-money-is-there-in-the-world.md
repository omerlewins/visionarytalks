# How Much Money Is in the World? Cash, M2 and Wealth (2026)

**By Daniel Mercer | Checked October 25, 2026**

There is no single answer, because "money" means four different things. Here is each one, with its date:

- **Physical cash:** about **$7.3 trillion** in notes and coins across the US, the euro area, China and Japan, from central bank data for August to October 2026.
- **Narrow money (M1):** about **$57 trillion** in those same four currency areas, cash plus checking and other instant-access deposits, August and September 2026.
- **Broad money (M2):** about **$106 trillion** in those four, and about **$151 trillion** for the whole world in 2024, the latest year the World Bank covers.
- **Total wealth:** about **$518 trillion** of personal net worth at the end of 2025, according to UBS. That counts homes, shares and pensions, not just money.

Most answers online mix these up. I keep them apart below, convert every foreign figure to US dollars at a dated exchange rate, and say where an estimate is mine rather than an official number.

## The money ladder

Each rung is a bigger and broader measure. Only the first four are money. The rest are what people often mean when they ask the question.

<div id="vt-chart-money-ladder"></div>

| Measure | US dollars (trillions) | As of | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Physical cash, four largest currency areas | 7.3 | Aug to Oct 2026 | Fed, ECB, PBoC, BoJ |
| Narrow money (M1), four largest areas | 57 | Aug to Sep 2026 | Fed, ECB, PBoC, BoJ |
| Broad money (M2, euro area M3), four largest areas | 106 | Aug to Sep 2026 | Fed, ECB, PBoC, BoJ |
| Broad money, whole world | 151 | 2024 | World Bank |
| Global debt | 365 | June 30, 2026 | IIF |
| Personal wealth, 56 markets | 518 | Dec 31, 2025 | UBS |
| OTC derivatives, notional value | 846 | June 30, 2025 | BIS |

The chart uses a log scale, so each step up the axis is a multiple, not an addition. You cannot add the rungs together. Cash is part of M1, M1 is part of M2, and much of M2 is counted again inside wealth.

## How much physical cash is there?

About $7.3 trillion in the four largest currency areas:

| Currency area | Cash in circulation | In US dollars | Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| United States | $2.49 trillion | $2.49 trillion | Week to Oct 21, 2026 |
| Euro area | €1.62 trillion | $1.88 trillion | Aug 31, 2026 |
| China (M0) | ¥15.02 trillion | $2.24 trillion | Sep 30, 2026 |
| Japan | ¥109.4 trillion | $0.70 trillion | September 2026 average |
| **Total** | | **$7.3 trillion** | |

Exchange rates are the Federal Reserve's noon rates for the end of each data month: $1.1617 per euro on August 31, 2026, 6.7045 yuan and 157.25 yen per dollar on September 30, 2026.

No official body adds up every country's cash. Every other currency would push the total higher, but I do not publish a world cash figure I cannot source. What the numbers do show is how small cash is: in these four areas it is about 7% of broad money.

## How much money is in bank accounts? M1 and M2

Most money is not cash. It is deposits that banks create when they lend. M1 adds checking and other instant-access deposits to cash. M2 adds savings and short-term time deposits. The euro area publishes M3, its closest equivalent, which also includes money market funds and short-term bank debt.

| Currency area | M1 (local) | M2 or M3 (local) | M2 or M3 in US dollars | Date |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| United States | $19.99 trillion | $23.34 trillion (M2) | $23.34 trillion | August 2026 |
| Euro area | €11.29 trillion | €17.65 trillion (M3) | $20.50 trillion | August 2026 |
| China | ¥116.76 trillion | ¥359.63 trillion (M2) | $53.64 trillion | September 30, 2026 |
| Japan | ¥1,081.9 trillion | ¥1,295.6 trillion (M2) | $8.24 trillion | September 2026 average |
| **Total** | | | **$105.7 trillion** | |

China holds about half of the total. That is not because China is richer than the US. Chinese households and companies keep far more of their savings as bank deposits, while Americans hold more in shares, bonds and funds that M2 does not count.

Growth is uneven. US M2 was up 5.7% in the year to August 2026, euro area M3 3.5% in August, China's M2 7.2% to September and Japan's M2 2.0% in September, each from its own central bank's release.

## How much money is in the world in total?

About **$151 trillion** of broad money in 2024. That is my calculation from two World Bank figures: world broad money of 135.7% of world GDP in 2024, and world GDP of $111.7 trillion in 2024. It is the latest year the World Bank covers for the world total, so treat it as a 2024 figure, not a 2026 one.

The four largest currency areas alone reached $106 trillion by September 2026. Add the UK, India, Canada, Korea, Brazil and the rest, and a 2026 world figure is very likely above the 2024 one. I would rather give you the dated official number than invent a precise 2026 total.

Spread across about 8.1 billion people in 2024, $151 trillion is roughly $18,600 each. Nobody actually holds that: the figure includes company deposits, and deposits are very unevenly shared.

## What is "the money in the world" if you count wealth?

If you mean everything people own, the number is far bigger. UBS's Global Wealth Report 2026, published June 30, 2026, splits the adults in its 56 markets into four wealth bands. Those bands add up to about **$518 trillion** of personal net worth at the end of 2025, after debts. UBS says those 56 markets hold more than 92% of the world's wealth. Wealth grew 10.8% in US dollar terms in 2025.

Wealth is not money. Most of it is houses, company shares and pension funds. You can sell a house for money, but the house is not money while you own it.

## Who owns 90% of the world's wealth?

Roughly the richest sixth of adults. In UBS's figures for the end of 2025:

| Wealth band (US dollars) | Share of adults | Wealth held | Share of wealth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Over $1 million | 1.5% (58 million) | $250.59 trillion | 48.4% |
| $100,000 to $1 million | 15.3% (588 million) | $200.72 trillion | 38.8% |
| $10,000 to $100,000 | 41.1% (1.58 billion) | $63.16 trillion | 12.2% |
| Under $10,000 | 42.1% (1.62 billion) | $3.22 trillion | 0.6% |

So the 16.8% of adults with more than $100,000 own about 87% of personal wealth. The 1.62 billion adults with less than $10,000 own 0.6% of it between them.

## What is the richest country?

It depends on the measure, so here are three, all from UBS for the end of 2025:

- **Most total wealth:** the United States, with 35.7% of the personal wealth in UBS's sample. Greater China is next with 18.5%.
- **Highest average wealth per adult:** Switzerland, at $910,382. The US is second at $696,277.
- **Highest median wealth per adult:** Luxembourg, at $394,005. The median is the better guide to what a typical adult has, because a few billionaires do not move it.

## How rich is the top 1%?

In the United States, the top 1% held **32.5% of household net worth**, about **$60.3 trillion**, in the second quarter of 2026, according to the Federal Reserve's Distributional Financial Accounts. That was up from 31.7% in the first quarter. For what it takes to join them, see [what it takes to be in the top 1%](/top-1-percent-net-worth/).

Worldwide, UBS does not publish a top 1% figure. Its closest measure is the millionaire band above: 1.5% of adults holding 48.4% of personal wealth at the end of 2025. For the very top of that group, see [the youngest billionaires](/youngest-billionaires/).

## Why debt and derivatives are bigger than all the money

Global debt passed **$365 trillion** at the end of June 2026, according to the Institute of International Finance's Global Debt Monitor, reported on September 23, 2026. It is bigger than broad money because one dollar of money can pay off many debts as it moves around.

Over-the-counter derivatives had a notional value of **$846 trillion** at the end of June 2025, the latest the Bank for International Settlements has reported. Notional value is the size of the contracts the payments are calculated on, not money that changes hands. The BIS put the actual market value of those contracts at $21.8 trillion.

Both numbers are often quoted as "all the money in the world." Neither is.

## What could change by 2027?

Broad money keeps rising in most economies as banks lend and governments borrow, so the 2026 world figure is very likely higher than 2024's. The dollar matters too. A weaker dollar makes every euro, yuan and yen figure above look bigger in dollar terms, even if nothing changed locally. I will update this page when the World Bank publishes a newer world figure and when central banks release new monthly data.
