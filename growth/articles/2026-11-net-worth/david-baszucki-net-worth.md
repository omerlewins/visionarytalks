## How much of Roblox does David Baszucki own?

Roblox has two classes of stock. Class A shares trade on the New York Stock Exchange and carry one vote. Class B shares carry 20 votes each and can be turned into Class A at any time. Baszucki is the only holder of Class B.

The 2026 proxy statement, with ownership as of February 13, 2026, lists:

| Holding | Shares | Who controls it |
|---|---|---|
| Class B, all outstanding | 47,070,464 | Baszucki (100% of the class) |
| of which: 2020 David Baszucki Gift Trust | About 12.8 million | Baszucki is trustee |
| of which: 2020 Jan Baszucki Gift Trust | About 12.4 million | Baszucki controls voting and investment |
| Class A | About 1.2 million | Baszucki |
| **Total** | **About 48.3 million** | **58.8% of total voting power** |

So his economic stake, roughly 48 million shares, is well under 10% of the company, but his voting control is a majority. That is why no outside shareholder can force a change at the top of Roblox, even after the stock's long slide.

Wikipedia still describes him as owning "about 13%," a figure from the 2020 filings before Roblox listed. The current proxy shows a smaller economic slice and a larger say.

Separately, his brother Gregory Baszucki, a Roblox director and cofounder of their first company, held about 12.1 million Class A shares (1.8%) on the same date and has been selling under a trading plan since July 2026.

## Where Baszucki came from

Baszucki was born in Winnipeg, Manitoba, on January 20, 1963, and grew up in Eden Prairie, Minnesota. He studied electrical engineering at Stanford and graduated in 1985.

His first company, Knowledge Revolution, came out of a simple idea: let students build physics experiments on a computer. Its Interactive Physics software sold to schools, and MSC Software bought the company for $20 million in December 1998, according to Wikipedia. Roblox grew out of the same idea. Baszucki and engineer Erik Cassel, who had worked with him at Knowledge Revolution, started building a 3D "physics playground" in 2003. Cassel died in 2013.

Roblox does not make most of its games. Users build them, and creators are paid from what players spend on Robux. Forbes says Roblox paid creators nearly $1 billion in 2024, and that players spent more than 73 billion hours on the platform that year.

## Controversies

Baszucki has faced repeated criticism over child safety on Roblox. In March 2025 he told parents, "if you're not comfortable, don't let your kids be on Roblox," a comment that drew wide pushback. In August 2025, after Roblox banned a YouTuber who had been exposing predators on the platform, a Change.org petition calling for his resignation passed 100,000 signatures. In December 2021, a New York Times investigation reported that he and relatives used a small-business tax break to avoid tens of millions of dollars in capital gains taxes.

## Philanthropy

He and his wife run the Baszucki Group, which funds mental health and bipolar disorder research. In September 2022 they joined Sergey Brin and Kent Dauten in a combined $150 million commitment to bipolar research. Forbes gives him a philanthropy score of 2 out of 5. In October 2026 the Washington Post reported that a Baszucki family fund gave $5 million toward the White House ballroom project.
