## The investigation: three filings in 18 years

I searched the full text of every Tesla filing on SEC EDGAR for "Holzhausen." It appears in three documents:

| Filing | Date | What it says about him |
|---|---|---|
| S-1 exhibit, Fifth Amended and Restated Investors' Rights Agreement | Filed Jan. 29, 2010 (agreement dated Aug. 31, 2009) | Lists "Franz von Holzhausen" in Exhibit E, the list of **Series E Stockholders**, with no share count. |
| Investor presentation, Exhibit 99.1 to an 8-K | Dec. 16, 2010 | Lists him as "Chief Designer" on the slide "Best of Silicon Valley and Auto." |
| Additional proxy materials | Oct. 20, 2025 | Tesla filed a transcript of his "Ride the Lightning" podcast interview urging shareholders to back Musk. "We need Elon, this doesn't happen without him, so go make that happen," he said. |

The first filing is the most useful finding. Exhibit E of Tesla's 2009 investors' rights agreement names him as a holder of Series E preferred stock, the private funding round Tesla raised in 2009 before its IPO. In other words, about a year after joining, he owned Tesla stock as an investor, not just through employee options. The exhibit lists names only, so it does not show how many shares he held or what he paid.

What is missing tells the rest of the story. He has never filed a Form 4, the form every Tesla officer and director must file within two business days of buying or selling Tesla stock. That means he is not treated as a Section 16 officer, and his stock activity is not public. Tesla's proxy statements report pay only for Musk and its top financial and operating officers, so his salary and equity grants have never been disclosed either.

The 2025 filing is the most revealing. Tesla used its chief designer as a public voice in its campaign for Musk's pay package and Master Plan Part IV, but still did not have to say what he is paid.

## What could he be worth?

The ingredients point to real wealth, but the amounts are unknown.

- **Timing.** He joined in August 2008, nearly two years before Tesla's June 2010 IPO at $17 a share, which is $1.13 after the 2020 and 2022 stock splits. Tesla's stock has risen many times over since then.
- **Pre-IPO stock.** The 2009 agreement lists him as a Series E stockholder, so he held preferred shares before the IPO, on top of any employee options.
- **Tenure.** Senior Tesla employees usually receive new stock grants every year. He has been there 18 years.
- **The unknowns.** How many options he received, how many he exercised, how many shares he sold, and how much he paid in tax are all private.

Because of those unknowns, I can't responsibly narrow it beyond this: if he kept his Series E shares and early grants, his Tesla stock alone could be worth tens of millions of dollars or more. The $10 million to $25 million figures on aggregator sites show no math, and I would not quote them as fact.

## Who is Franz von Holzhausen?

Von Holzhausen grew up in Simsbury, Connecticut, the son of a designer. He spent two years at Syracuse University before transferring to ArtCenter College of Design in Pasadena, where car designers for most major automakers train. At Volkswagen he worked on the Concept One, the show car that became the New Beetle. At General Motors he worked on the Pontiac Solstice and Saturn Sky roadsters, and at Mazda he led concept cars such as the Kabura and Furai.

Musk hired him in 2008, when Tesla was a small startup selling a sports car built on a Lotus chassis. His first job was the Model S, the car that made Tesla a real automaker. He has led design on every Tesla since, including the Cybertruck.

He is married to Vicki von Holzhausen, a former car designer who runs her own company making sustainable leather alternatives under the von Holzhausen name. They have two children, according to Wikipedia.
