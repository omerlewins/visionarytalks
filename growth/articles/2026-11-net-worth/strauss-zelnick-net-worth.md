## Why Strauss Zelnick's pay looks like $1

Zelnick is not technically a Take-Two employee. Take-Two hires his firm, ZMC, under a management agreement, and ZMC supplies Take-Two's CEO (Zelnick) and president (Karl Slatoff). Each of them takes $1 a year from Take-Two. The real pay goes to ZMC as fees and stock.

The proxy sets out how ZMC can split the money: Zelnick may receive no more than 60% of what ZMC is paid, and Slatoff no more than 40%. The actual split is up to ZMC.

The jump in the pay ratio, from 1.5 to 1 to 467 to 1, is what happens when the SEC disclosure counts the ZMC fees he can receive rather than his $1 salary. Take-Two's 2022 agreement with ZMC, which replaced a 2017 one, covered fiscal 2026.

In past proxies, much of ZMC's pay has come as restricted Take-Two stock tied to the share price and to Take-Two's results, so a successful GTA VI launch would show up in ZMC's payouts over the next few years. Those grants are held by ZMC, not by Zelnick personally, which is one reason his personal share count looks small next to his influence.

## Who is Strauss Zelnick?

Zelnick, 69, is a media executive and private equity investor. He studied at Wesleyan University and earned an MBA and a law degree from Harvard, according to Wikipedia. Before games, he ran parts of the film and music industries: three years at Vestron, four years as president and COO of 20th Century Fox, and then president and CEO of BMG Entertainment, the proxy says.

He founded ZelnickMedia in 2001 to buy and run media companies. In 2007 his firm won a proxy fight for control of Take-Two, which was then struggling with an options backdating scandal and losses. He became chair that year and CEO in 2011. Under his watch Take-Two released Grand Theft Auto V and Red Dead Redemption 2 and bought mobile game maker Zynga in 2022.

Outside work, he is known for his fitness routine and wrote a book on aging well, "Becoming Ageless."
