## The investigation: why there is no number

For most big-company executives, a net worth estimate starts with two filings: the proxy statement, which shows pay, and Form 4, which shows every share bought or sold. Neither exists for Spencer.

**Pay.** A US public company must report pay only for its CEO, CFO and three other most highly paid executive officers. Microsoft's fiscal 2025 annual report lists its executive officers, and Spencer is not among them. The list covers people such as CFO Amy Hood, President Brad Smith and Chief Marketing Officer Takeshi Numoto. So Microsoft's proxies never showed what he was paid, even though he reported directly to Satya Nadella from September 2017.

**Shares.** Only officers covered by Section 16 of the securities law file Form 4s. I searched SEC EDGAR's full-text search for Form 4 filings naming "Phil Spencer" and found none. That fits his status: without officer status, his stock sales were never public.

**Forbes and Bloomberg.** Neither lists him. Their lists start at $1 billion, and nothing suggests he is near that.

So every figure you see online is built on assumptions about what an executive vice president at Microsoft earns. That is a reasonable starting point, but the actual amounts were never published.

## What he likely earned, and what we cannot see

Microsoft pays its senior executives mostly in stock that vests over several years. Spencer was a corporate vice president from about 2009, head of Xbox from 2014, an executive vice president from 2017 and a division CEO from 2022. Over that time, Microsoft's stock rose many times over, so any shares he kept would have grown sharply in value.

What we cannot see is how much stock he was granted, how much he sold, or what he paid in tax. Those three numbers decide whether his fortune is closer to the low or high end of the online estimates, and none of them are public.

## The retirement

On February 20, 2026, Microsoft announced that Spencer would retire. Asha Sharma, president of Microsoft's CoreAI product and a former chief operating officer of Instacart, became executive vice president and CEO of Microsoft Gaming, reporting to Nadella. Xbox president Sarah Bond, long seen as his likely successor, left the company, and studio chief Matt Booty became chief content officer.

Spencer wrote to staff that he had told Nadella the previous fall that "stepping back and starting the next chapter of my life" was on his mind. He stayed on as an adviser through the summer of 2026. In July 2025, an Xbox spokesperson had denied a rumor that he was leaving, saying "Phil is not retiring anytime soon," according to reports at the time.

## Who is Phil Spencer?

Spencer grew up in Ridgefield, a small town in southwest Washington, and studied technical and scientific communication at the University of Washington. He got his first Microsoft internship in 1988 after a chance meeting with a college classmate's father, a Microsoft vice president, he told Axios.

He spent his first decade on consumer software before moving into games in 2001, running Microsoft's European game studios. He became head of Microsoft Studios in 2008, which put him in charge of franchises such as Halo, Gears of War and Forza. When Nadella became CEO in 2014, one of his early moves was to give Spencer the whole Xbox business, shortly after the troubled launch of the Xbox One.

Under Spencer, Xbox shifted from selling consoles toward Game Pass subscriptions and putting its games on PC and rival consoles. Players knew him by his gamertag, P3, and for wearing game-themed shirts on stage. He lives in the Seattle area with his wife and two daughters.
