# Jamie Siminoff Net Worth 2026: What the Filings Show About Ring's Founder, From Amazon to Latch and Back

**By Daniel Mercer | Checked November 24, 2026**

Jamie Siminoff's net worth is not public, and the $300 million to $500 million figures that circulate online are not backed by any filing. Amazon paid about $839 million, net of cash acquired, for Ring in April 2018, according to its 10-K, but Siminoff's share of that price was never disclosed.

Public filings show smaller pieces: about $3.8 million of 2024 pay from Latch, which then bought back 80% of the 19.1 million Latch shares he received for nominal consideration, $330,624 in Jabil board fees for fiscal 2025, and stakes averaging 43% in 14 real estate partnerships. He returned to Amazon in April 2025 to run its home security business.

## The Ring sale

Siminoff launched the video doorbell company as Doorbot in 2013 and renamed it Ring in September 2014, according to Wikipedia. On Shark Tank in 2013 he asked for $700,000 for a company he valued at $7 million, and turned down Kevin O'Leary's offer.

Ring went on to raise more than $200 million from investors including Kleiner Perkins, Qualcomm Ventures, Goldman Sachs, DFJ Growth and Richard Branson, Wikipedia says. Each round diluted the founders, and Amazon had become an investor too before it bought the company.

Amazon's 2018 10-K says it acquired Ring on April 12, 2018, "for cash consideration of approximately $839 million, net of cash acquired." Press reports at the time put the price above $1 billion; Wikipedia cites about $1.15 billion. Neither Amazon nor Ring disclosed how much went to Siminoff, and no filing we found shows his stake at the sale.

He stayed at Amazon as Ring's CEO until March 2023, when he stepped down to become its "chief inventor," and Elizabeth Hamren took over, according to Wikipedia.

Latch, a smart lock and building software company, bought Siminoff's startup Honest Day's Work on July 3, 2023, and he joined as chief strategy officer. His shares were locked until July 2028, and Latch could buy them back for nominal consideration if he left before then, his Form 4 says.

Latch was behind on its SEC reports during a long financial restatement and could not grant him stock, so from November 2023 it paid him an extra $1.55 million a year in cash, for $1.75 million a year in all, the proxy says. He was also granted 8 million performance options in August 2024 that would have vested only if Latch's stock reached $4 to $6. They were forfeited.

He separated from Latch on December 31, 2024. Under a November 18, 2024 agreement Latch repurchased 80% of his consideration shares, 15,260,540 of them, at $0.0000508 a share, or about $775 in total, his Form 4 shows. After his advisory role ended in May 2025, Latch repurchased about 800,000 more and released about 200,000 from restrictions, the proxy says. That leaves him with about 3 million Latch shares, our count, worth about $540,000 at the $0.18 close on November 23, 2026.

## Real estate and board seats

Latch's 2026 proxy also discloses that Siminoff is a limited partner in 14 entities that own properties managed by a Latch subsidiary. He owns between 4% and 97% of them, 43% on average, and the properties have been cash flow positive since he invested, the proxy says. Their value is not disclosed.

He sat on the board of Jabil, the electronics manufacturer, from 2024 and earned $330,624 there in fiscal 2025, including 1,300 restricted stock units. He held 2,600 Jabil shares in the December 2025 proxy and did not stand for re-election in January 2026. Jabil disclosed that it had made less than $20 million of products for Amazon's home security business in fiscal 2025.

## Who is Jamie Siminoff?

James Siminoff graduated from Babson College in 1999 and received an honorary degree there in 2021, according to the college. He built the first version of his video doorbell in his garage, and he has estimated that his 2013 Shark Tank appearance, where he took no deal, brought Doorbot $5 million of extra sales, according to Wikipedia.

He ran Ring through its sale to Amazon and for five years after, left in 2023 for Latch, and was back at Amazon by April 2025 as vice president of product overseeing Ring and the rest of its home security business. His book, Ding Dong: How Ring Went From Shark Tank Reject to Everyone's Front Door, came out in November 2025.
