## The investigation: 18 filings, then silence

Srouji's SEC record has a clear start and end. His Form 3, filed December 28, 2015, marked his promotion to senior vice president, which made him a reporting officer. Over the next 32 months he filed 17 Form 4s. The last was filed on August 21, 2018. A full-text search of EDGAR shows nothing from him after that, including after his April 2026 promotion.

That means Apple stopped treating him as one of its Section 16 officers in 2018. Since then, his stock grants, vesting and any sales have been private, and Apple's proxy does not report his pay.

## The retention story

In December 2025, Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reported that Srouji had told Tim Cook he was seriously considering leaving Apple, according to MacRumors and Fortune. Days later, Srouji told his team he was not planning to leave anytime soon.

On April 20, 2026, Apple named him chief hardware officer, adding hardware engineering, which John Ternus had run, to his chip and hardware technology group. Ternus is set to succeed Cook as CEO on September 1, 2026. "Johny is one of the most talented people I have ever had the privilege to work with," Cook said in Apple's announcement. 9to5Mac called keeping him one of Ternus's biggest challenges. Apple has not said whether the promotion came with a new stock grant.

His name has come up elsewhere too. Intel considered him for its CEO job in 2019, and in November 2026 he was among the names floated for Nvidia's top job, according to Wikipedia.

## Who is Johny Srouji?

Srouji grew up in the Abbas neighborhood of Haifa, the third of four children of a carpenter who made casting molds for the Israeli defense ministry. A high school teacher who also taught at the Technion introduced him to computers, and he went on to earn bachelor's and master's degrees in computer science there, with honors. He speaks Arabic, Hebrew, French and English.

After processor design jobs at Intel and IBM, he joined Apple in 2008 to build the A4, the first chip Apple designed itself, used in the first iPad and the iPhone 4. Every A-series chip since, the M-series chips that replaced Intel processors in Macs from 2020, and Apple's own cellular modems came from his group. He also set up Apple's research center in Herzliya, Israel, which Wikipedia calls its second largest in the world.
