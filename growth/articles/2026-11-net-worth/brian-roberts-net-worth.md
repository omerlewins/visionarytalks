## How much of Comcast does Brian Roberts own?

Comcast has two voting share classes. Class A trades on Nasdaq. Class B is held entirely by Roberts, carries 15 votes a share, and is set up so that it always has 33 1/3% of the combined vote no matter how many Class A shares exist. That fixed slice is why Comcast's buybacks and stock issuance never dilute his control.

His September 3, 2026 Form 4 lists his Class A holdings after a gift of 101,900 shares on September 2:

Each Class B share can be converted into one Class A share, so I value both classes at the Class A price. That is conservative: a buyer would likely pay more for the Class B block because it carries control.

The same filing shows 382,860 restricted stock units settling on September 1, 2026 at $26.30. Roberts had chosen to defer those shares into another company investment plan, so they did not land in his share count.

### Why Forbes says $1.5 billion

Forbes does not publish its math. The difference between $773 million in stock and Forbes' $1.5 billion most likely reflects what he has accumulated outside his current Comcast stake: four decades of salary and bonuses, decades of share sales and dividends, the deferred compensation he notionally invests elsewhere, and any shares he received when Comcast spun off its cable networks as Versant in January 2026. None of those are totaled in a public filing, so I treat Forbes' figure as reasonable but unverifiable.

## Who is Brian Roberts?

Brian Roberts grew up in Philadelphia, where his father Ralph built Comcast from a single cable system in Tupelo, Mississippi, bought in 1963. Brian started working there as a teenager, interning at 15, and joined full time after graduating from the University of Pennsylvania's Wharton School. He became president at 31 in 1990, and in 1997 his father handed him most of the family's voting shares.

As CEO from 2002 he turned a regional cable company into one of the world's largest media groups. He bought AT&T's cable business in 2002, NBCUniversal in stages from 2011, and Sky in Europe in 2018, and Comcast owns the Philadelphia Flyers hockey team. In January 2026 he made president Mike Cavanagh co-CEO and spun off most of the cable TV networks into a new company, Versant.

Outside work, Roberts is a ranked squash player who won gold for the US team at the 2005 Maccabiah Games, according to Forbes. He is married with three children.
