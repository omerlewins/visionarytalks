# Anduril Stock: Can You Buy It, IPO Plans and the $94.80 Share Price

**By Daniel Mercer | Checked November 2, 2026**

No, you cannot buy Anduril stock on a public exchange. Anduril Industries is private and has not filed for an IPO. In October 2026 it opened a tender letting employees sell up to about $1.3 billion of shares at $94.80 each, which Anduril confirmed values the company at about $90 billion. That is up from $61 billion in its $5 billion Series H in May 2026. CEO Brian Schimpf said in July that Anduril was not rushing into an IPO.

The defense company was founded in 2017 and is based in Costa Mesa, California. It makes autonomous drones, the Fury unmanned fighter, counter-drone systems and the Lattice software that ties them together.

At $94.80 a share and a $90 billion valuation, Anduril has roughly 950 million shares on a fully diluted basis. That is my estimate from the two reported numbers, not a company figure.

## Can I buy Anduril stock before an IPO?

Not through a normal brokerage account. Your realistic options are:

1. **Wait for an IPO.** Palmer Luckey has said he wants to take Anduril public eventually, but nothing has been filed, and Schimpf said in July 2026 that the company was not rushing.
2. **Secondary platforms and SPVs.** Accredited investors sometimes find Anduril shares or fund units on private marketplaces, but Anduril controls transfers, and its own tenders are how most employees sell. SEC EDGAR also lists several special purpose vehicles named for Anduril, such as MCF Anduril LLC, Mana Anduril 2, LP and Anduril Fund, LLC, which pool accredited investors' money to buy into the company. They are not affiliated with Anduril, and their fees and terms vary.
3. **Venture and crossover funds.** Some publicly traded funds and interval funds hold small private stakes, including in defense tech. Check a fund's latest holdings before buying it for Anduril exposure.
4. **Public defense stocks.** Anduril competes and partners with listed contractors such as Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman and General Dynamics, but none of them gives direct exposure to Anduril.

Why no IPO yet? An October tender lets employees cash out without the pressure of a daily share price, which Schimpf told CNBC on October 15 keeps the company focused on "a massive, unprecedented ramp in defense production."
