# Who Owns Monster Energy? Coca-Cola, Index Funds and Two South Africans

**By Daniel Mercer | Checked October 25, 2026**

Monster Energy is made by Monster Beverage Corporation, a public company traded on Nasdaq as MNST, so it is owned by its shareholders. The biggest is The Coca-Cola Company, with about 20.9% as of February 2026. Rodney Sacks and Hilton Schlosberg, who took control of the company in 1990, together control about 8.1%. Schlosberg has been sole CEO since June 13, 2025, and Sacks is chairman.

Coca-Cola does not own Monster. It is a large minority shareholder with one seat on the board, and its bottlers distribute Monster drinks around the world. Monster runs itself from Corona, California.

## Who owns Monster Energy today?

The largest holders, from Monster's 2026 proxy statement (filed March 27, 2026):

| Shareholder | Shares | Stake | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Coca-Cola Company | 204,243,204 | 20.9% | Per its filing of January 28, 2025; nominates one director |
| Hilton H. Schlosberg | 75,575,906 | 7.7% | CEO and vice chairman |
| Rodney C. Sacks | 73,445,410 | 7.5% | Chairman |
| The Vanguard Group | 63,682,206 | 6.5% | Index and mutual funds |
| Brandon Limited Partnership No. 2 | 58,773,888 | 6.0% | Sacks and Schlosberg are its general partners |
| BlackRock | 50,718,228 | 5.2% | Index and mutual funds |
| All directors and officers together | 79,753,759 | 8.1% | As of February 13, 2026 |

Sacks' and Schlosberg's numbers overlap. Both men are counted as owners of the same family partnerships, including the Brandon partnerships, which is why each shows more than 7% but together they control 8.1%, not 15%. Monster had 978.1 million shares outstanding at the end of 2025.

## Does Coca-Cola own Monster?

Partly. Coca-Cola paid Monster a net $2.15 billion in cash for about 16.7% of the company in a deal that closed on June 12, 2015, per the two companies' joint announcement. In the same deal, the companies swapped brands: Monster took Coca-Cola's energy drinks, including NOS, Full Throttle and Burn, and Coca-Cola took Monster's non-energy drinks, including Hansen's Natural Sodas, Peace Tea and Hubert's Lemonade. Coca-Cola became Monster's preferred distributor worldwide.

Coca-Cola's stake grew to 20.9% without it buying more, because Monster has been buying back and retiring its own shares. At the October 23, 2026 share price, the stake is worth about $9.4 billion, a little more than four times what Coca-Cola paid.

## Every Monster CEO

| CEO | Years as CEO | Background | What changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rodney C. Sacks | November 1990 to January 2021 | South African lawyer; led the group that took control in 1990 | Ran the company for 30 years as sole CEO; launched Monster Energy in 2002 and renamed the company Monster Beverage in 2012 |
| Rodney Sacks and Hilton Schlosberg (co-CEOs) | January 2021 to June 12, 2025 | Schlosberg was president, COO and secretary from 1990 and CFO from 1996 to 2021 | Bought Bang Energy out of bankruptcy for $362 million in 2023 |
| Hilton H. Schlosberg | June 13, 2025 to today | Director since November 1990; vice chairman | Sacks moved to chairman; net sales reached $8.29 billion in 2025 |

*Sources: Monster Beverage 2026 proxy statement; Monster Beverage 2025 10-K; Bloomberg Law, July 12, 2023.*

The same two men have run the company for 36 years. Monster's proxy says Sacks stays an employee, directing marketing, innovation and litigation, through December 31, 2026.

## Who founded Monster Energy?

No single founder. The Hansen family business dates to the 1930s, but Monster as it is today was built by Rodney Sacks and Hilton Schlosberg, two South Africans who took control of the company in 1990 and launched the Monster Energy drink in 2002.
