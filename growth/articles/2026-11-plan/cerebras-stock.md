# Cerebras Stock (CBRS): IPO Price, Who Owns Cerebras and the OpenAI Warrant

**By Daniel Mercer | Checked November 2, 2026**

Yes, you can buy Cerebras stock. Cerebras Systems listed on Nasdaq under the ticker CBRS on May 14, 2026, at $185 a share, and closed its first day at about $311. The IPO raised $6.4 billion. Its biggest holders at the IPO were Fidelity (11.0%), Benchmark (9.5%) and Foundation Capital (8.3%), and OpenAI holds a warrant for up to 33.4 million shares. The stock later fell below its IPO price, to a reported low near $147 in October 2026.

The numbers below come from Cerebras' own SEC filings unless I say otherwise.

Cerebras has three share classes. Class A shares were sold to the public, Class B shares are held mostly by pre-IPO holders and insiders, and Class N shares are non-voting.

## Does OpenAI own part of Cerebras?

Not yet outright, but it can. As part of a large computing deal, Cerebras issued OpenAI a warrant to buy up to 33,445,026 Class N shares at $0.00001 each. The warrant vests in tranches as milestones are met, including OpenAI funding a roughly $1.0 billion working capital loan in January 2026, Cerebras delivering contracted computing capacity, and certain market capitalization targets.

If fully vested, those shares would equal about 14% of the 237.6 million shares outstanding in August 2026 (my calculation). Cerebras recognized $56.8 million of OpenAI revenue in the second quarter of 2026.

G42, the Abu Dhabi technology group, was Cerebras' biggest customer before OpenAI. G42 provided 85% of revenue in 2024 and 24% in 2025, while the Abu Dhabi university MBZUAI provided 62% in 2025. G42 also holds a fully vested warrant for 1,857,516 Class N shares.

Research and development spending rose more than fivefold to $320.2 million in the quarter, which is why losses widened after the IPO. Cash and cash equivalents were $6.74 billion on June 30, 2026, up from $702 million at the end of 2025.

## What happened to Cerebras stock after the IPO?

The stock opened far above its IPO price and closed its first day near $311, but it gave back those gains over the following months. Reports in October 2026 said it fell below the $185 IPO price as early lock-up releases let pre-IPO holders sell, reaching about $146.98 on October 12, 2026, roughly 62% below its first-day high. Cerebras says its lock-up releases pre-IPO shares in tranches before the standard 180-day period ends.
