# Colette Kress Net Worth 2026: Nvidia's Interim CEO and Her $830 Million Stake

**By Daniel Mercer | Checked November 12, 2026**

Forbes estimated Colette Kress's net worth at $1.2 billion as of June 3, 2026, when she debuted on its list of America's richest self-made women. Nearly all of it is Nvidia stock. Her last Form 4 (Sept. 17, 2026) shows 4,775,733 Nvidia shares held directly, in trusts, through an LLC and by family members, worth about $830 million at Nvidia's $173.73 close on Nov. 11, 2026, down from about $1.14 billion at the October 6 high. On Nov. 12, 2026, Nvidia named her interim CEO after Jensen Huang resigned; she also stays CFO.

That makes Kress, 59, the first person other than Huang to run Nvidia since he cofounded it in 1993. Unlike most people on the Forbes lists, she got rich on stock earned as an employee, not as a founder.

## Is Colette Kress the CEO of Nvidia now?

Yes, on an interim basis. In a Form 8-K filed on Thursday, Nov. 12, 2026, Nvidia said Jensen Huang "resigned as President, Chief Executive Officer, and a member of the Board, effective immediately," and that Kress "has agreed to serve as Interim Chief Executive Officer." She keeps her jobs as executive vice president and CFO "until a permanent successor has been named."

The same filing made longtime director Harvey C. Jones, a former CEO and executive chairman of Synopsys who joined Nvidia's board in 1993, the company's first-ever chair of the board. The board says it will consider internal and external candidates for the permanent job.

"The Board has full confidence in Colette and the leadership team," Jones said in the release. Kress's own line: "Our focus remains on serving our customers, partners, developers and shareholders."

Nvidia did not give a reason for Huang's departure. It followed a week of reports about his personal relationships, including one with a businesswoman tied to an Nvidia customer whom U.S. officials reportedly suspect of working for Chinese intelligence. Huang apologized on Nov. 8 for being "unfaithful to my wife," CNBC reported, and the board opened an independent investigation. Yahoo Finance reported the board said it had no reason to believe trade secrets or national security were compromised.

### How long will Kress be interim CEO?

Nvidia set no end date. Wedbush analyst Dan Ives told CNBC he expects her to hold the role for about a year, with an outside hire more likely for the long term. Names analysts floated on Nov. 12 included Kress herself, Ian Buck (head of hyperscale), Bryan Catanzaro (applied deep learning), CTO Michael Kagan and chief scientist Bill Dally. President Trump posted that Apple's Tim Cook should get the job. None of them has been named a candidate by Nvidia.

Her first big test comes fast: Nvidia reports fiscal third-quarter results on Nov. 17, 2026, against her own August guidance of $108 billion in revenue, plus or minus 2%.

## Who is Colette Kress?

Colette M. Kress is a career finance executive who spent nearly 25 years at Texas Instruments, Microsoft and Cisco before Nvidia. She holds a bachelor's degree in finance from the University of Arizona and an MBA from Southern Methodist University's Cox School of Business.

At Microsoft, where she spent 13 years, she ran finance for the Server and Tools division, the business that became Microsoft's cloud and enterprise software arm. At Cisco she was senior vice president and CFO of its Business Technology and Operations Finance organization.

### Why she joined Nvidia

When Huang hired her in 2013, Nvidia was mostly known for gaming graphics cards. In a 2024 Fortune interview, cited by the Globe and Mail, she described Nvidia as the smallest company she had ever worked for. Forbes and IBTimes report that her two sons, then young gamers who knew Nvidia's chips, endorsed the move; she has said Huang's whiteboard pitch of where the company was going sold her on it.

### What she does at Nvidia

As CFO she runs Nvidia's finance organization, and she is the voice on Nvidia's quarterly earnings calls next to Huang. Analysts know her well because she gives the guidance. "AI is no longer a nice-to-have. AI is now a necessity for enhancing productivity across all industries and roles," she told investors on the May 20, 2026 earnings call, per Fortune.

Under her, Nvidia's annual revenue went from the low single-digit billions to $215.9 billion in fiscal 2026, and it returned about $26 billion to shareholders in fiscal Q2 2027 alone, with $99 billion left on its buyback authorization. In August 2026 she told investors Nvidia expected revenue to grow about 70% in fiscal 2028, while calling that outlook supply-constrained.
