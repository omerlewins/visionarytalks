# Who Owns Raising Cane's? Founder Todd Graves, With About 92%

**By Daniel Mercer | Checked October 25, 2026**

Raising Cane's is a private company owned mostly by its founder, Todd Graves, who holds about 92%, according to Forbes. Co-founder Craig Silvey sold out in 1999 for a six-figure sum and now owns less than 0.5%. Raising Cane's is not publicly traded and has no private equity owner. Graves is co-CEO alongside AJ Kumaran, who has shared the job since 2017. Forbes estimated Graves' net worth at about $20 billion on October 25, 2026.

That makes Raising Cane's unusual among big chains. Wingstop, Burger King and Jersey Mike's all passed through private equity owners. Graves has kept control for 30 years and owns almost all of his restaurants rather than franchising them.

## Who owns Raising Cane's today?

| Owner | Stake | Source and date |
|---|---|---|
| Todd Graves | About 92% | Forbes, December 2025 (CNBC reported "more than 90%" in October 2024) |
| Craig Silvey, co-founder | Less than 0.5% | Forbes, December 2025 |
| Others | The remaining roughly 8% | Not publicly identified |

The company does not publish its ownership, so these figures come from Forbes, which said it reviewed company financials. Forbes did not name the holders of the remaining stake.

Almost all of Graves' wealth is his Raising Cane's stake. Forbes also reported in December 2025 that the company had paid him an estimated $250 million dividend. These are Forbes estimates; the company has never been sold or valued in a public market.

At $6.6 million a store in 2024, a Cane's restaurant took in more than three times the $2.0 million average at a US Wingstop and more than four times the $1.4 million at a Jersey Mike's, based on those companies' 2025 filings.

## Is Raising Cane's publicly traded?

No. Raising Cane's has no stock on any exchange, and Graves has not announced any plan to sell shares. The company does carry debt, about $2.6 billion net, Forbes reported in December 2025.
