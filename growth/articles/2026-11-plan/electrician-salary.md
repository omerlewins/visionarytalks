# Electrician Salary 2026: Pay in Every State, City and Industry

**By Daniel Mercer | Checked October 27, 2026**

Electricians in the US earned a median of $63,190 a year in May 2025, or $30.38 an hour, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Oregon pays the most, with a median of $101,310, and Arkansas the least, at $49,070. The top 10% earned more than $108,510. BLS projects 9% job growth from 2025 to 2035, with about 72,700 openings a year.

Where you work matters more than almost anything else. An electrician at a power utility earned a median of $106,640, while one at a typical building contractor earned $61,640. These figures are straight-time wages; BLS says overtime is common in the trade and it is not included.

## How much do electricians make?

BLS counted 757,220 electrician jobs in May 2025. Their pay spread:

|---|---|---|

*Source: BLS OEWS, May 2025, occupation 47-2111, from the BLS national data file downloaded October 27, 2026.*

The average sits about $8,300 above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up. The state and industry tables below show where they are.

## Apprentice, helper and senior electrician pay

BLS does not publish pay by license level, so there is no official journeyman or master electrician figure. The closest measures are the jobs on either side of an electrician:

| Job (BLS title) | Jobs | Median, May 2025 | Average |
|---|---|---|---|
| Powerhouse, substation and relay electrical repairers | 20,720 | $103,020 | $100,760 |
| Electrical power-line installers and repairers (linemen) | 131,070 | $95,320 | $91,970 |
| First-line supervisors of construction trades | 812,210 | $79,920 | $86,450 |
| **Electricians** | **757,220** | **$63,190** | **$71,490** |
| Helpers, electricians | 63,630 | $42,670 | $44,080 |

*Source: BLS OEWS, May 2025, national estimates. The supervisors line covers every construction trade, not only electricians.*

Most electricians learn through a 4- or 5-year apprenticeship, with about 2,000 hours of paid on-the-job training each year, according to BLS. Helpers, at a median $42,670, give a rough idea of pay at the start. Moving into substation work or line work pays roughly $30,000 to $40,000 more at the median. For line work, see our [lineman salary](/lineman-salary/) guide.

About three in four electricians work for building equipment contractors, the firms that wire homes, offices and factories. The best-paid large employers are power utilities ($106,640), carmakers ($92,250) and local governments ($82,120). A small group at natural gas utilities earned a median of $131,780.

## Job outlook for electricians

BLS projects employment of electricians to grow 9% from 2025 to 2035, from 821,000 to 896,900 jobs, much faster than the average for all occupations. It expects about 72,700 openings a year. BLS points to two drivers: linking new power sources such as solar and wind to the grid, and the build-out of data centers for AI.

## How to become an electrician

A high school diploma is the usual starting point. Most electricians then learn through an apprenticeship, though some start at a technical school, which can count toward the apprenticeship. Most states require electricians to be licensed, according to BLS.

## How I checked these numbers

Every pay and job figure comes from the BLS May 2025 Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics data files, which I downloaded on October 27, 2026, for occupation 47-2111. The outlook and training details come from the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook. BLS wages exclude overtime and benefits.
