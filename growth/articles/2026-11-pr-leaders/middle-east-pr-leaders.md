# Top PR Agencies in Dubai and the Middle East (2026): The 17 Leaders Who Run Them

**By Daniel Mercer | Checked November 24, 2026**

The Middle East's public relations business is run by two kinds of people. One group runs the regional arms of global networks: Burson, Edelman, Weber Shandwick, APCO, Ogilvy and MSL. The other group built agencies from nothing in Dubai, Jeddah and Riyadh, and several of them now win the same government and blue-chip briefs as the networks.

This guide profiles 17 of those leaders, each with the agency they run, how they got there, and what the public numbers say about the business. Where an agency has disclosed revenue or growth, we give the figure and the year. Where it has not, we say so instead of guessing. Most of the financial figures come from PRovoke Media's annual Best Agencies in EMEA reviews (the May 2026 edition covers 2025), which is the only regular source in which Middle East agencies report revenue and growth.

The short version: Burson is the largest PR firm in the region, APCO is the largest that discloses a regional revenue number (almost $53 million in 2025), and the fastest growth is at younger network offshoots like Ruder Finn Atteline and Current Global, both up more than 60% last year.

## How the Middle East PR market works

Three things set the region apart from London or New York.

**Governments are the biggest clients.** Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030 program, the giga-projects, Abu Dhabi's sovereign funds and Dubai's tourism and economy departments buy more strategic communications than any private sector. Burson told PRovoke that nearly half its regional business is in Saudi Arabia. APCO's client list runs from the Saudi Ministry of Tourism to the World Government Summit. Edelman has advised Mubadala for 16 years and is the global agency of record for the Saudi Ministry of Culture. That is why the most valuable skills here are public affairs, crisis and "national narrative" work, not just consumer launches.

**The networks consolidated, and the leadership turned over.** In 2024, WPP merged BCW and Hill & Knowlton into Burson, which retired the ASDA'A BCW name that had dominated the region for two decades. Omnicom's takeover of Interpublic, completed on November 26, 2025, put Weber Shandwick, Current Global, Golin and FleishmanHillard under one owner. Between April 2025 and May 2026, Burson, APCO and Edelman all changed their top Middle East leader.

**Independents are winning on creativity.** The 2026 PRovoke list of the 15 best agencies in the Middle East includes ten firms that are not owned by an advertising holding company. Gambit, Brazen MENA and The Romans have all won multinational retainers that used to go to networks by default.

## The network leaders

### 1. Fouad Bou Mansour, Burson

**Role:** CEO, Middle East, North Africa and Turkey, since late 2025.

Burson announced Bou Mansour's appointment on September 2, 2025, after Bashar AlKadhi said in April 2025 that he would retire as regional CEO. Bou Mansour came from the Saudi Tourism Authority, where he spent two years as vice president of its creative studio. Before that he was chief operating officer of Impact BBDO Group in the UAE for seven years, overseeing Impact BBDO, FleishmanHillard and Impact Porter Novelli with a team of 115. He studied computer science at the Lebanese American University in Beirut.

**The agency:** Burson Middle East is the old ASDA'A BCW and Hill & Knowlton combined. PRovoke's 2025 review called it the region's largest PR agency by revenue, team size and footprint, with 475 people across six offices, more than 250 clients and 51 new clients won in 2024. Its work includes the Royal Commission for AlUla, COP16 in Riyadh, Emirates NBD and Visa.

**Analysis:** Bou Mansour inherited a business that had just gone through a merger and a CEO change in the same year. PRovoke's 2026 review describes the agency as having "stabilised" under him, with strong retention and growth among top accounts, but it gave no revenue figure. His agency side and client side experience in Saudi tourism is exactly the profile a firm with half its business in the Kingdom would want. The open question is whether Burson can keep its scale lead while smaller rivals grow two to six times faster.

### 2. Justin Westcott, Edelman

**Role:** CEO, Middle East, and chief operating officer for EMEA, since May 2026.

Edelman named Westcott to the Middle East job in May 2026, in the same announcement that made Julian Payne CEO of EMEA. Westcott joined Edelman in 2006 and has been global chair of technology and UK COO. He previously worked in the Middle East and ran the Dubai office. He replaced Omar Qirem, who left to become a communications advisor to the Abu Dhabi Investment Council, according to PRovoke. Day to day, the region is run by David Kingsmill-Moore (managing director, UAE), Nidaa Lone (head of Abu Dhabi) and Alex Salem (COO, Saudi Arabia).

**The agency:** Edelman's Middle East fee income grew 29% in 2024 and another 29% in 2025, while the firm worldwide fell about 4% to $950 million. In 2024 the Middle East was already 13% of Edelman's EMEA revenue, second only to the UK. Its financial communications arm, Edelman Smithfield, quadrupled in the region over three years. Clients include Mubadala, ADNOC, the Abu Dhabi Department of Finance, M42, DP World (its largest EMEA client) and the Saudi Ministry of Culture.

**Analysis:** On disclosed numbers, Edelman is the best performing global firm in the region. Two years of 29% growth while the parent shrank makes the Middle East one of the most important regions in the company, which explains why it now has an EMEA operating chief running it directly.

### 3. Ziad Hasbani, Weber Shandwick

**Role:** Regional CEO, Middle East, North Africa and Turkey. He was already CEO of Weber Shandwick MENA in 2015, and he also oversees Current Global and Jack Morton in the region.

**The agency:** Weber Shandwick has offices in the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Kuwait. It told PRovoke it wins two out of every three pitches it enters and keeps 91% of its top clients, with an average relationship of more than six years. Its clients include Mastercard, General Motors, Ericsson, IBM, McDonald's, Google Cloud, Ikea and Hilton. It ran the Disney Abu Dhabi theme park announcement, which produced more than 10,000 pieces of coverage in 48 hours.

**Analysis:** Hasbani is the longest serving network chief on this list, and that stability shows in the awards. Weber Shandwick MENAT was named Large Agency of the Year at the 2025 MEPRA Awards and Large Agency Index Champion in Earned First's first MENA Consultancy Performance Index in February 2026. It does not publish regional revenue, so its size relative to Burson and APCO cannot be checked.

### 4. Samer El Hachem and Mamoon Sbeih, APCO

**Roles:** El Hachem became president of APCO's MENA business in February 2026. Sbeih, who ran the region for more than a decade, became chairman of MENA and APCO's chief international business development officer. Faten AlMasri is deputy president.

El Hachem comes from management consulting. Sbeih's new job focuses on linking the Gulf and China, with clients such as the carmaker Chery and Alibaba, according to PRovoke.

**The agency:** APCO opened in Dubai in 2006 and bought the regional network JiWin in 2010. It now has offices in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Riyadh, Amman, Doha and Manama. Its Middle East fee income passed $50 million in 2024, up 15%, and was almost $53 million in 2025, which PRovoke described as down slightly. Worldwide, APCO earned just under $240 million in 2025, so the Middle East is a little over a fifth of the firm. It also owns Pomelo, a creative studio with about 75 people.

**Analysis:** APCO is the only big firm that discloses a Middle East revenue number, and at about $53 million it is very likely one of the two largest PR businesses in the region alongside Burson. It is closer to a policy and government advisory firm than a classic PR agency, which is why its leadership pick came from consulting rather than media.

### 5. Ashraf Shakah, Memac Ogilvy PR

**Role:** President, Ogilvy PR MENA, as named in PRovoke's 2025 review.

**The agency:** Ogilvy's PR business trades in the region as Memac Ogilvy, with hubs in Dubai and Abu Dhabi. PRovoke reported growth of more than 25% in 2024, driven by new business from Experience Abu Dhabi, Kenvue and Majid Al Futtaim, joining clients such as Qatar Foundation, StarzPlay and the car brand Omoda.

**Analysis:** Memac Ogilvy made the 2025 top 10 but not the 2026 top 15, and no 2025 growth number has been published. It remains one of the largest WPP communications businesses in the region, but on public evidence it is no longer setting the pace.

### 6. Maclean Brodie, MSL

**Role:** CEO, MSL Middle East, since 2023.

**The agency:** MSL is Publicis Groupe's PR network, with offices in Dubai, Beirut, Cairo, Jeddah and Riyadh. It grew 45% in 2025 from what PRovoke called a modest base, won 24 new pieces of business including PepsiCo, Pernod Ricard, Siemens and Talabat, and renewed 16 clients including Lego, ADNOC and the Saudi Tourism Authority.

**Analysis:** Brodie's job has been to turn what PRovoke described as a publicity support function inside an ad group into a standalone consultancy. A 45% jump and a place in the 2026 top 15 suggest it is working, though MSL still does not disclose the size of the base.

### 7. Peter Jacob, Current Global

**Role:** Managing director, Middle East, since the firm entered the region in 2023. He moved over from sister agency Weber Shandwick.

**The agency:** Current Global's Middle East revenue grew 80% in 2024 and more than 60% in 2025, and headcount rose more than 50% last year. Its "Heinz Ketch-up and Down" campaign ranked as the fifth most awarded PR campaign in the world in PRovoke's Creative Index, and the agency was the most awarded agency for its size at the MEPRA Awards with seven golds. It was also the overall champion in the February 2026 MENA Consultancy Performance Index. Clients include Riyadh Air, Dubai Airports, TikTok, New Balance and Dubai Tourism.

**Analysis:** Jacob has built the clearest network start-up success story in the region. The model is a boutique with the backing of a holding company. Because it sits under Hasbani's regional umbrella, Omnicom now owns both the most established and the fastest growing network PR shops in the Gulf.

### 8. Sophie Simpson, Ruder Finn Atteline

**Role:** Managing director, Ruder Finn Atteline MENA. She founded Atteline in Dubai in 2016 after moving to the city in 2013 as an account director.

**The agency:** Ruder Finn bought Atteline in 2023 and renamed it Ruder Finn Atteline in 2025. PRovoke reported revenue up more than 60% in 2025, client numbers up 36%, the team doubled, and 31 new clients including American Express, Netflix, Xiaomi, Sony, Chalhoub Group and Dubai Future Foundation. It has hubs in Dubai and Riyadh.

**Analysis:** Simpson is the rare founder who sold to a global firm and kept running the business. Growth of more than 60% puts Ruder Finn Atteline level with Current Global as the fastest growing agency in PRovoke's 2026 Middle East list.

## The independent founders

### 9. Mohamed Al Ayed, TRACCS

**Role:** Founder and CEO. He founded the firm in Jeddah in 1998 after working in PR at Samarec and SPRC, and rebranded it as TRACCS in 2001. He studied modern languages at Eastern Oregon University in the US.

**The agency:** TRACCS has 170 professionals across 11 owned and affiliate offices in Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Egypt, Kuwait, Lebanon, Oman, Qatar, Turkey and the UAE. PRovoke says it is the only firm from the region on its list of the world's 200 biggest PR firms by fee income. It is backed by a partnership with Accordience, the parent of Grayling. Clients include Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, DHL, Boursa Kuwait, Chevron, Siemens and Mercedes-Benz. The group also runs the Athar creative festival in Saudi Arabia, an events company called arc and a training arm called Enrich. Majdi Al Ayed is executive vice president and Shereen Bushehri is deputy chief executive.

**Analysis:** Al Ayed is the most important Arab founder in the industry. TRACCS was built on the idea that the region needed its own PR industry run by Arabic speakers. It does not publish revenue, but its global top 200 ranking and its 11-office footprint make it the largest locally owned PR network in the Middle East by any public measure.

### 10. Chris Christodoulou, Action Global Communications

**Role:** Group CEO. He took over from his father, Tony Christodoulou, who founded the firm in Cyprus in 1971 and opened its first UAE office in Dubai in 1994. Tony Christodoulou has since died. Euan Megson is managing director for MENA.

**The agency:** Action Global has more than 180 people across its network, which also covers Eastern Europe and Central Asia. In 2024 its revenue rose 26% and pre-tax profit 45%, and Qatar revenue grew 42%. It reopened in Riyadh in 2025. Clients include Dubai's Department of Economy and Tourism, Dubai World Trade Centre, Qatar Airways, Revolut, Samsung and Booking.com. It was named Best Midsize Agency in the 2026 MENA Consultancy Performance Index.

**Analysis:** Action is the oldest agency on this list and one of the very few family-owned firms of its size. Thirty years in Dubai gives it the deepest institutional client list of any independent, especially in tourism and events.

### 11. Jamal Al Mawed, Gambit Communications

**Role:** Founder and managing director. Al Mawed is an Emirati-British communications specialist. He sits on the MEPRA strategy board and advises the mass communication program at the American University of Sharjah.

**The agency:** Gambit is based in Dubai and covers the GCC and Levant. Revenue rose 39% in 2024, its fifth year, and about 30% in 2025, when headcount reached 50 for the first time. In 2025 it reorganised into four divisions (Pulse, Sage, Atelier and FWD) under a plan it calls Gambit 3.0. Clients include Samsung, BMW, Porsche, Amazon, Red Bull, Lego, Uber and Mandarin Oriental. It was MEPRA Medium Agency of the Year in 2023 and PRCA Medium Agency of the Year in 2024.

**Analysis:** Al Mawed's selling point is selectivity. The agency says it turned down more than 100 tenders in a year that did not fit its values. Two straight years of roughly 30% to 40% growth with full client retention make Gambit the strongest UAE-founded independent of its generation.

### 12. Louise Jacobson, Brazen MENA

**Role:** Founder and managing partner. She spent nearly a decade at Brazen in the UK before launching Brazen MENA in Dubai in 2015.

**The agency:** Brazen MENA earned $3 million in 2024, up 31%, and $3.5 million in 2025, up 25%, all from organic growth. It has 30 people across Dubai, Riyadh, Cairo and Amman, and its senior leadership team is all women. Retainers are 85% of revenue. Clients include Bacardi, Hilton, Jack Daniel's, Veuve Clicquot, SKIMS, Abercrombie & Fitch and Raffles & Fairmont Doha.

**Analysis:** Brazen is one of only two independents on this list to publish an exact revenue figure, which makes it a useful benchmark: a well regarded 30-person Dubai consultancy earns around $3.5 million a year. Jacobson's niche is luxury, hospitality and real estate reputation work.

### 13. Joe Lipscombe, The Romans

**Role:** Partner leading The Romans in the Middle East since 2024. He previously led Ogilvy's PR business in MENA. Sophia Boudjemaa, formerly of MSL, joined as the agency's first regional managing director.

**The agency:** The London agency opened in Dubai in 2022, and its Middle East business took off after Lipscombe arrived. It made £2.5 million in the region in 2024, nearly 13% of its global revenue, and opened in Riyadh in early 2025. Clients include Audi, Binance, Porsche, Wynn Resorts, the Abu Dhabi Grand Prix and the Madinah Development Authority.

**Analysis:** The Romans shows how fast a creative-first agency can scale in the Gulf with the right local leader. It went from no regional clients to four multi-market retainers within six months of Lipscombe's arrival.

## Saudi Arabia's homegrown leaders

### 14. Ibrahim Al-Mutawa, Jummar PR

**Role:** Co-founder and CEO of Jummar PR & Communications, founded in Riyadh in 2021. Before PR, he was a Riyadh correspondent for Reuters and a Saudi correspondent for Forbes Arabia.

**The agency:** Jummar works for Saudi brands and events, including the music festival company MDLBEAST. Al-Mutawa was named by PRWeek among the Middle East's most influential PR professionals in 2023 and was on Campaign Middle East's first 40 over 40 list in 2026.

**Analysis:** Al-Mutawa represents the new generation of Saudi agency owners: former journalists who know the domestic press and can offer Saudi clients a Saudi-run alternative to the networks.

### 15. Abdulrahman Abed, Legends PR

**Role:** Founder and CEO of Legends Public Relations and Communications, founded in Riyadh in 2019.

**The agency:** Legends was appointed the strategic international PR partner for Riyadh Season, the Kingdom's flagship entertainment calendar, according to The Business Year.

**Analysis:** Winning Riyadh Season's international media work is one of the largest event mandates in the region, and it went to a young local firm. That is a sign of how much Saudi government buyers now favour Saudi-founded agencies.

## The founder who built the template

### 16. Sunil John, ASDA'A

**Role today:** Senior advisor, MENA, at Stagwell, and founder of ASDA'A, according to Gulf Business's 2026 list of Indian business leaders in the Gulf.

John founded ASDA'A in Dubai in 2000 as an independent agency. WPP bought a majority stake in 2008, and the firm became ASDA'A Burson-Marsteller and later ASDA'A BCW, with John as president of BCW for the Middle East from 2018. He also created the ASDA'A Arab Youth Survey, which the agency describes as the largest study of young Arabs and which reached its 15th edition in 2023 and is regularly cited by global media. He left the agency after 24 years, and in 2024 ASDA'A BCW was folded into the new Burson.

**Analysis:** No one did more to make Dubai the region's PR capital. ASDA'A was one of the first agencies founded in the Gulf to reach multinational scale, and its alumni include Burson's previous regional CEO, Bashar AlKadhi, who was chief operating officer of ASDA'A Burson-Marsteller.

### 17. Rupert Young, Brunswick Gulf

**Role:** Senior partner and head of Brunswick Gulf, according to Brunswick's website.

**The firm:** Brunswick is a corporate and financial advisory firm rather than a PR agency, but it handles many of the region's IPOs, takeovers and boardroom crises. It has offices in Dubai and Abu Dhabi and opened in Riyadh in 2022. It does not publish regional revenue and was not in PRovoke's ranking, which covers agencies rather than advisory firms.

## How to choose a PR agency in Dubai or Riyadh

Based on what the agencies themselves emphasise, the fit depends on the job:

- **Government, policy or a sovereign client:** APCO, Burson, Edelman and Weber Shandwick have the deepest public affairs and crisis benches. Global Think Group in Riyadh focuses only on strategy and policy work.
- **A Saudi launch:** TRACCS, Jummar and Legends are Saudi-founded. Most international firms now have Riyadh offices too, but check whether the team is based there or flies in from Dubai.
- **Consumer and luxury brands:** Gambit, Brazen MENA, The Romans, Current Global and Ruder Finn Atteline have built their reputations on brand work and creative campaigns.
- **Financial communications and IPOs:** Edelman Smithfield, Brunswick and APCO's capital markets team.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the largest PR agency in the Middle East?

Burson, according to PRovoke Media's 2025 review, which called it the region's largest PR agency by revenue, team size and footprint, with 475 people. APCO is the largest that discloses a regional revenue figure: almost $53 million in fee income in 2025.

### What are the top PR agencies in Dubai?

PRovoke Media's 2026 list of the 15 best agencies in the Middle East includes Action Global, APCO, Brazen MENA, Burson, Current Global, Edelman, Ethicore, Gambit, Global Think Group, Golin, MSL, Ruder Finn Atteline, The Romans, TRACCS and Weber Shandwick. All of them except Global Think Group and TRACCS have their main regional office in Dubai.

### Who is the CEO of Burson Middle East?

Fouad Bou Mansour, CEO for the Middle East, North Africa and Turkey since late 2025. He replaced Bashar AlKadhi.

### Who is the CEO of Edelman Middle East?

Justin Westcott, named in May 2026. He is also Edelman's chief operating officer for EMEA. He replaced Omar Qirem.

### Who founded ASDA'A BCW?

Sunil John founded ASDA'A in Dubai in 2000. The agency was merged into Burson in 2024, and John is now a senior advisor at Stagwell.

### What is the largest Arab-owned PR agency?

TRACCS, founded by Mohamed Al Ayed in Jeddah in 1998. It has 170 people in 11 offices and is the only Middle East firm in PRovoke's global top 200 by fee income.

### How much do PR agencies in Dubai make?

Few disclose it. Brazen MENA, a 30-person independent, earned $3.5 million in 2025. The Romans earned £2.5 million in the region in 2024. At the top end, APCO's Middle East business earned almost $53 million in 2025.
