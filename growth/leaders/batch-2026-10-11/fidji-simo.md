## The move was an organizational appointment

[OpenAI’s May 7, 2025 announcement](https://openai.com/index/leadership-expansion-with-fidji-simo/) named Simo CEO of Applications, reporting to Sam Altman. It described a transition from Instacart and explained how the role fit alongside research and compute responsibilities. This is the company’s account of the appointment, not an independent description of compensation.

The brief says she later moved to a part-time advisory role. We have not verified the original July 2026 statement. The header therefore dates the appointment rather than presenting the 2025 role as confirmed current employment. A newer first-person or company record is needed to resolve that transition.

A leadership profile should preserve this temporal distinction. An announcement describes a particular moment. It cannot safely stand in for an indefinitely current role. The same problem applies to equity awards: a grant date, vesting date and departure date may imply very different financial positions.

## The proxy supplies a stronger financial trail

The [2026 Instacart proxy](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1579091/000157909126000024/cart-20260409.htm) says Simo left the CEO role on August 15, 2025. It identifies the unvested portions of performance-based and time-vesting awards as forfeited. It separately says a director award was forfeited after her board departure on November 24.

Those are financially material facts. A granted award can be reported in a compensation table before the recipient has an unconditional right to every share. Continued service and performance requirements matter. A departure can eliminate prospective value that a headline compensation figure appears to assign.

Our analysis is that the correct unit is the award’s status, not the compensation headline. To reconstruct retained equity, a researcher must separate vested shares, unvested awards, exercised options and sold holdings. The proxy directly rules out counting some unvested awards as current property.

## Salary rate is not salary received

The proxy lists a $1 million annual base-salary rate for Simo in its 2025 salary table. That is an annual rate, not proof she received a full year of salary after leaving in August. The relevant paid amount would come from the appropriate compensation disclosure and employment period.

The document also describes annual RSU arrangements and vesting terms. Grant-date accounting value can differ from a later realized value. Market movements, vesting and taxes affect what someone eventually receives. Adding grant values across years would not produce a personal asset total.

Our calculation: no total personal fortune is calculated from the salary rate or grants. The ledger records the rate and forfeiture events with their definitions. That gives readers useful financial information without disguising incomplete ownership data as an estimate.

## A holdings reconstruction needs transaction records

The brief proposes a Form 4 trail. Such filings can help identify transactions and remaining reportable holdings for specified dates. They must be reconciled chronologically and read with their ownership footnotes. One sale does not disclose how all proceeds were used or what liabilities remain.

A public-stock ledger is still only one part of personal wealth. It may exclude private investments, property and debt. OpenAI compensation could introduce a different private instrument with its own restrictions. We do not fill those gaps with a typical executive stake or an assumed compensation package.

The profile therefore has no numerical wealth chart. Its financial timeline instead shows appointment and departure-related events. That design avoids plotting a salary rate beside unvested equity as if they formed a wealth history. Unknown net worth is visibly labeled, rather than encoded as zero.

## An equity award is a conditional claim, not a bank balance

The [Instacart proxy](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1579091/000157909126000024/cart-20260409.htm) identifies two departure events and awards forfeited at each. It is more useful than adding grant-date values in a compensation table. A grant can have service or performance conditions. If those are not met, the original disclosed value does not establish an asset still owned.

Our analysis is that an executive wealth ledger should distinguish grants, vesting, settlement, retention and disposal. Grant-date fair value is a compensation-accounting observation. Vesting changes the award’s conditions. A sale changes the asset held, but gross proceeds do not establish a current cash balance. Conflating these stages can overstate what remains.

The annual base-salary rate requires similar care. It is a rate, not a promise that twelve months of pay were received. Departure timing and other compensation terms can matter. We do not sum the annual rate with forfeited awards and label the result personal net worth.

Her [OpenAI appointment](https://openai.com/index/leadership-expansion-with-fidji-simo/) establishes a dated role, not an employment-package value. An agreement or disclosed allocation would be needed for that part of the ledger. The [Greg Brockman profile](/leaders/greg-brockman/) addresses a different observation: a reported statement about a founder stake.

## What the financial record reveals

The distinctive finding is the explicit forfeiture language. It shows why compensation awarded is not automatically compensation retained. An investigator who reads only the summary table can materially overstate a former executive’s present equity exposure.

A useful update would reconcile the last retained Instacart holdings and verify the later OpenAI role. The current article already supplies the important accounting distinction. It leaves the personal total open because the required asset-and-liability inputs remain private or unreviewed.

## Independent research and methodology

We have not reconciled all Form 4 transactions or verified the later advisory-role statement. No compensation sum is presented as a net-worth estimate.
