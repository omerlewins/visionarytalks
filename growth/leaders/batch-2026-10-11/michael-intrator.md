## Public ownership still requires careful reconciliation

Public filings can reveal more than private-company estimates, but they are not automatically a complete wealth model. An ownership schedule can include direct holdings, entities, convertible securities and shares subject to specified rules. Dates and footnotes determine what can be counted together.

The April 2026 proxy provides a later ownership snapshot, including indirect holdings. We have reviewed its table and footnote, but have not completed the subsequent Form 4 reconciliation. It supplies a historical starting point, rather than an October 2026 share balance.

Our calculation: the proxy’s 61,505,714-share beneficial-ownership total is about 11.63 times its 5,289,944 Class A component. This compares two fields in one dated filing. It does not reconcile a third-party wealth tracker or establish exclusive economic ownership. A personal model must reconcile transaction dates and distinguish economic ownership from voting control before assigning a market value.

## Why a tracker can show a much smaller total

An insider tracker can display a subset of reportable securities rather than a complete fortune. Depending on its method, it may emphasize direct holdings, one share class or recent transactions. A small displayed amount therefore does not by itself contradict a provider’s broader estimate.

That is a methodological possibility, not a verified explanation of a tracker that includes only one share class. We have not reviewed the tracker’s underlying records and inclusion rules. We do not name a particular missing trust or class as the cause without performing the reconciliation.

Likewise, sale proceeds do not automatically remain as cash. A transaction filing can establish what was sold and at what reported price. It generally does not establish how proceeds were subsequently invested, donated or used to meet liabilities. A public-equity ledger and total personal wealth have different scopes.

## The ownership footnote changes the picture

The [April 2026 proxy](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962826000191/crwv-20260422.htm) reports 5,289,944 Class A and 56,215,770 Class B shares beneficially owned as of April 15. Its footnote includes 24,633,894 Class B shares in Omnadora Capital LLC, 4,576,000 in one family trust and 2,290,320 in another. It includes spouse holdings and eligible awards. This is broader than directly registered shares.

Our calculation: the reported class totals sum to 61,505,714 beneficially owned shares. That is approximately 11.63 times the reported Class A count alone. This identifies a scope problem; it does not reconstruct a wealth provider’s model. SEC beneficial ownership need not equal personal economic entitlement to every included asset.

The proxy reports 38.70% of voting power. Class B has ten votes per share and Class A has one. Voting influence and economic value require separate treatment. A voting percentage should not be multiplied by market capitalization to estimate a fortune. Securities counts and rights are the starting inputs.

The snapshot is historical. Options, vesting, sales and transfers after April can change quantities. A current reconstruction would follow subsequent filings, apply a dated price and examine other assets and liabilities. We do not claim a specific website missed exactly this amount without reviewing its coverage. The original footnote establishes why a narrow tracker can be incomplete.
