# Leaders Content Playbook

Oct 11, 2026 · @Omer

How Visionary Talks writes "Who is \[Name\]?" and "\[Name\] net worth" profiles that rank, get cited by AI answers, and add something the internet doesn't already have.

## What a winning page does

A Leaders page wins when it answers the search in the first 50 words, answers every follow-up question a reader has, and contains at least one fact no other page has. The current SERPs for these names are news recaps, Wikipedia and net-worth content farms. None of them show their sources, date their numbers, or read the filings. That is our edge.

**Every page must have:**

- **A dated answer up top.** Name, role, net worth figure, source, date. One caveat at most.
- **Quick facts.** Age, nationality or background, education, company, role, co-founders. These are the "related searches" people type next (age, parents, Indian, education, wife). If we don't answer them, a news site will.
- **One or two information-gain findings.** A filing detail, a court record, a reconciled estimate, an early-career artifact. Named in the brief as "must-do". Non-negotiable.
- **Every wealth estimate we can find, dated and side by side.** Forbes, Bloomberg, Hurun, sworn testimony. Then explain why they differ.
- **A capital ledger.** Every funding round, deal or filing that moved their wealth: date, amount, valuation, source.
- **A real chart.** Three or more dated points. A single dot is not a chart; leave it out until there's a series.
- **Sources with links and access dates.** Six or more for a full profile. Primary records first.
- **Author box, methodology and a correction link.** Already in the template; keep them.
- **Links to the company's "Who owns" page, sibling profiles and the salary page.** See the interlinking section.

## Page structure and subtitles

Use this order on every profile. Subtitles must contain a name, company, number or query a person would search. Abstract labels like "The asset changes even when the founder story continues" rank for nothing; save that voice for the body.

| # | Section | Subtitle pattern (H2) | What goes in it | Length |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Title + hook | H1: Who is \[Name\]? \[Specific hook\] | Hook names the company or the event, e.g. "The founder SpaceX paid $60B for" | 1 line |
| 2 | Header cards | (no heading) | Company / role · Stake status · Net worth + source + date · "Company value is separate" | 4 cards |
| 3 | The short answer | The short answer | 2-3 sentences: who, the dated estimate, one caveat. Must contain "\[Name\] net worth" | 50 words |
| 4 | Quick facts | \[Name\] at a glance | Table: age, born, nationality/background, education, company, role, co-founders, family where public and relevant | 6-10 rows |
| 5 | Origin | Before \[Company\]: \[what they did\] | Documented early work: competitions, papers, school records, first companies, first jobs | 200-400 words |
| 6 | Company story | How \[Company\] began | Founding date, co-founders, pivots. Label investor or company accounts as interested sources | 200-400 words |
| 7 | The bet | Why \[Company\] \[won / bet on X\] | The person's own contemporaneous words, then "My read:" in first person | 200-300 words |
| 8 | Capital ledger | \[Company\] funding history: $X raised across N rounds | Table of rounds, deals, filings + "Our calculation" line | Table + 100 words |
| 9 | Information gain | \[The finding, stated plainly\], e.g. "What SpaceX's 8-K says about the Cursor deal" | The brief's must-do finding with the record, what it shows and what it doesn't | 300-500 words |
| 10 | Net worth | \[Name\] net worth in \[Year\] | Every dated estimate side by side, why they differ, what's undisclosed | 200-300 words |
| 11 | Net worth timeline | \[Name\] net worth over time: \[first year\] to \[current year\] | Every dated estimate I can find, charted by source, with the event that moved each one. Spec below | Chart |
| 12 | FAQ | Questions people ask about \[Name\] | 4-6 short H3s from People Also Ask and related searches: Is X a billionaire? How old is X? Where is X from? Does X still own \[Company\]? | 40-60 words each |
| 13 | Methodology | How I researched this | 3-4 sentences specific to this page | 80 words |
| 14 | Sources | Sources and records | Every link opened, with access date, interested sources labeled | List |
| 15 | Follow | Follow the company / Related leaders | Who owns \[Company\], salary page, 2-3 sibling profiles | Cards |

Target 1,800-2,500 words. Merge or drop a section before padding it. One point made once beats the same caveat repeated across four sections.

### The net worth timeline

This is the section most competitors can't copy, because it takes digging. A single current figure is what every page has. A dated history of estimates, each tied to the event that moved it, is ours.

**Where to find historical estimates:**

1. **Wayback Machine captures of the Forbes and Bloomberg profile pages.** Each capture shows the real-time figure on that date. This is the richest source and almost nobody uses it.
2. **Forbes list editions:** Billionaires (April), Forbes 400 (September), 30 Under 30, country lists. Each is a dated, citable figure.
3. **Bloomberg Billionaires Index** profile and news stories quoting it on a date.
4. **Hurun** lists (India, Global) with their data cutoff dates.
5. **News articles that quote a figure with a date.** Cite the original estimator, not the outlet.
6. **Sworn testimony or filings** that value a stake on a date (e.g. Brockman's "nearly $30B" on May 4, 2026).

**How to build it:**

- One row per observation: date, figure, estimator, what changed since the last point (round, deal, IPO, share price move).
- Chart each estimator as its own series (Forbes, Bloomberg, Hurun), with points only on dates that have an observation. Don't draw one line blending sources.
- Before the person was estimated at all, write "Not estimated". Never plot a zero or a guess.
- Mark key events on the time axis (funding rounds, acquisition close, IPO).
- Below the chart, two or three sentences in first person on what the timeline shows: the jump, the gap between estimators, what drove it.
- Minimum three observations. If I find fewer, I publish the dated table and skip the chart.

**Example (Aman Sanger):** Forbes \~$1.3B before the SpaceX deal · Forbes $2.7B projected and Bloomberg $5.5B on Jun 16, 2026 · deal closes Aug 14, 2026 · Forbes $2.6B on Sep 23 · Forbes $2.8B on Oct 11. Two series, five points, one event marker. That chart tells a story the single $2.8B dot can't.

## How to bring new information

New information almost always comes from a primary record that the ranking pages didn't open. Before writing, spend the first research block on records, not articles. Ask: what document would a financial journalist pull for this person?

| Record type | Where to find it | What it gives a profile | Example from this series |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SEC filings (S-1, 8-K, 10-Q, proxy, 13D/13G, Form 4) | sec.gov EDGAR full-text search | Exact share counts, voting power, sales, deal terms | SpaceX 8-K: 389,289,254 shares for Cursor |
| SEC Form D and Form C | EDGAR full-text search for the company and for "\[Company\] SPV" | Real round sizes, investor counts, retail SPVs | Databricks Form D: $4,999,997,255 from 136 investors |
| UK Companies House | find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk | Share registers, PSC control bands, director changes, old company names | DeepMind's 2012 return: Suleyman held 157,500 shares |
| Court records and trial exhibits | CourtListener, PACER, court press releases | Sworn testimony on stakes, diaries, emails | Brockman's "nearly $30B" testimony |
| Competition and academic records | imo-official.org, IOI stats, arXiv, university theses | Verifiable early achievements, often misreported | Sualeh Asif: one IMO bronze, not three medals |
| Company announcements and terms of service | Company blog, press page, legal pages | Legal entity names, acquisitions, round leads | Windsurf customers contracted with Exafunction, Inc. |
| Government records | Budgets, gazettes, sovereign fund releases | State investors nobody mentions | Albania's budget act funding Thinking Machines |
| Wealth estimates | Forbes real-time, Bloomberg Billionaires, Hurun | Dated figures to compare and reconcile | Forbes $2.8B vs Bloomberg $5.5B for Aman Sanger |
| Archives | Wayback Machine, old Hacker News threads, early personal blogs | Origin artifacts | Amjad Masad's open-source REPL post |
| Their own words | Podcasts, conference talks, their X/blog posts, old interviews | Contemporaneous strategy, in quotes under 15 words | Truell's 2023 Hacker News comments on the VS Code fork |

**Four reliable information-gain moves:**

1. **Reconcile the estimates.** When Forbes and Bloomberg disagree, show both with dates and explain the gap (stake assumption, valuation date, method). Nobody else does this.
2. **Correct a common myth.** Check the claim every page repeats against the original record (medal counts, degrees, sale prices).
3. **Quantify from a filing.** Pull an exact number from a document and show the arithmetic as "My calculation", with what it is not.
4. **Find the missing asset.** A second company, a fund, a stake in another startup, a trust or LLC. Most profiles count one company only.

If research turns up no new record, the page isn't ready. Go back to the record types above before writing.

## Writing rules

### Voice: first-person investigator

Every page is written as Daniel Mercer, an investigator walking the reader through the records. First person makes the research visible, and visible research is the trust signal Google and AI answers reward.

- **Say what I did:** "I pulled SpaceX's 8-K." "I checked every Wayback capture of his Forbes profile." "I couldn't find a Form D under that name."
- **Say what I think, labeled:** "My read:" or "Here's what I make of it:" before analysis. Facts stay neutral; judgments are mine and clearly marked.
- **Say what I couldn't confirm:** "I couldn't verify his stake. Neither Forbes nor Bloomberg publishes its share count." Once, then move on.
- **Show the math as mine:** "My calculation: 25% × $15.8B ≈ $3.9B. That's the Sierra stake alone, not his net worth."
- **Only real steps.** "I" describes research actually done: documents read, databases searched, numbers calculated. Never claim an interview, a call, a source contact or access we don't have.
- **Investigator, not diarist.** Use "I" to move the reader through evidence, not to tell personal stories. Two or three "I" sentences per section is plenty; the records stay the subject.

**Before and after:**

| Third person (old) | First-person investigator (new) |
| --- | --- |
| The filing separately identifies 1,752,426 shares relating to vested restricted-stock units. | I went through SpaceX's closing 8-K line by line. It lists 1,752,426 shares for vested employee awards, separate from the 389 million paid to shareholders. |
| Our analysis is that a coding tool shortened the distance between building and using the product. | My read: the team built a tool they used every day, so they hit its problems first. |
| The records reviewed do not establish his exact stake. | I couldn't find his exact stake in any filing. Here's what the records do show. |

### General rules

- **Answer, then qualify.** Lead every section with the fact. Put the caveat in one sentence after it, once per page per caveat.
- **Separate three numbers always:** personal net worth, company valuation, deal consideration or cash proceeds. Never blend them.
- **Date and source every figure** in the sentence itself: "Forbes estimates $2.8 billion as of October 11, 2026."
- **Label interested sources:** "according to a Cursor investor", "the company says".
- **Show your math** as "My calculation:" with inputs, the arithmetic, and one line on what it is not.
- **Say what's unknown plainly** ("his stake has not been disclosed") and move on. Don't build sections around what we won't claim.
- **No internal language on the page.** Never "the brief", "verification notes", "research draft" or placeholder text in published copy.
- **Quotes under 15 words, one per source.** Paraphrase the rest.
- **Plain, specific sentences** under 25 words. Numbers, names and dates over adjectives. No hype words (visionary, genius, revolutionary). No em dashes.
- **Be fair on sensitive topics.** Lawsuits as allegations with docket numbers; health only as the person stated it publicly; politics only from records (FEC), no opinion.

### Interlinking

The site has four hubs: Leaders, Ownership, Salaries, Money. Every Leaders page should connect to the other hubs so authority flows both ways.

1. **Profile to company (required):** the "Who owns \[Company\]?" card near the top and again in "Follow the company". If the page doesn't exist yet, create a brief for it; don't leave the card empty.
2. **Company back to profile (required):** each founder named on a "Who owns" page links to their profile on first mention and in a "Follow the people" block.
3. **Profile to profile:** co-founders always link to each other (Truell, Sanger, Asif). Add 1-2 related leaders in the same space (same investors, same deal, same rivalry).
4. **Profile to salary page:** link the company's AI Salary Database page where it exists ("What Cursor pays its engineers").
5. **Hub pages:** every profile is listed on the Leaders hub; the hub groups them by company cluster (Cursor, OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepMind, AI coding, AI infrastructure).
6. **Anchor text:** descriptive and varied. Use the target's keyword naturally ("who owns Cursor", "Michael Truell's net worth"). Never "click here" or "this file".
7. **Limits:** one link per target per page; 5-12 internal links in the body is the healthy range.
8. **Publish in clusters:** release co-founders and their company page in the same week so no link points at nothing.

## Pre-publish checklist and refresh rules

**Before publishing:**

- [ ] Short answer has the keyword, figure, source and date in the first 50 words
- [ ] Quick facts table answers age, background, education, role
- [ ] At least one information-gain finding from a primary record, with a link to that record
- [ ] All available wealth estimates shown with dates, and the gap explained
- [ ] Capital ledger has every known round or deal
- [ ] Net worth timeline has every dated estimate I could find, one series per estimator, events marked (3+ points or a table only)
- [ ] Subtitles contain names, companies, numbers or queries
- [ ] No caveat repeated more than once
- [ ] No internal words on the page ("brief", "draft", "verification notes")
- [ ] Six or more linked sources with access dates
- [ ] FAQ answers 4-6 real People Also Ask questions
- [ ] Title tag, meta, slug, schema and canonical set as above
- [ ] Links to and from the company's "Who owns" page, co-founders and salary page
- [ ] Written in first person; every "I did" claim is a real research step. No em dashes

**Refresh rules:**

- Re-check Forbes and Bloomberg figures every quarter; update the chart with the new point rather than replacing the old one.
- Refresh within a week of any IPO, funding round, acquisition, lawsuit ruling or role change.
- Every refresh updates the visible "Research checked" date and dateModified in schema.
- When a confidential filing goes public (e.g. Anthropic's S-1), replace estimates with the filing's numbers the same week. That is the moment to outrank everyone.
